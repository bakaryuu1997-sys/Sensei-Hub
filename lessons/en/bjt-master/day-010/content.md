# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 10
## 🎯 Theme: Visitor Reception [Raiki Outai] - Guiding Etiquette and the Secrets of Seating Hierarchy
### 📊 Target Level: J3 -> J2+ (Professional Foundation)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Elevators - The "Blind Spot" of Politeness

You are guiding an extremely important partner to a meeting room on the 10th floor. The elevator arrives, you quickly hold the door and say: **"Osaki ni douzo"** (Please, go ahead).

Congratulations, you've just committed a "fatal" mistake in Business Japanese! Why?

In Japanese rules, an elevator is not just a means of transportation; it is a "hierarchical space." If you let the guest enter first and then squeeze in afterward to press the buttons, you have unintentionally forced the guest into the **"Shimoza"** (lower seat) position—near the control panel, which is the servant's position.

A J1+ level pro knows: You must enter first, hold the "Open" button, stand in front of the control panel, and invite the guest to the safest position deep inside. Japanese hospitality lies in this point: **"I will take responsibility for the operation so that you can be at ease."**

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: A Game of Go on the Office Floor

Arranging seating **"Sekiji"** in a meeting room or restaurant is like a game of Go. Every position has its own "value."
- **"Kamiza"** (Upper Seat): This is the position of power, furthest from the entrance, the quietest, and often with the best view. It's like the "King" on a chessboard.
- **"Shimoza"** (Lower Seat): This is the service position, closest to the door, and most likely to be disturbed. It's like a "Pawn" ready to jump into action (opening the door, ordering food).

If you seat a partner's Director in the **"Shimoza"**, you are sending a sub-textual message: "We disrespect your status." Conversely, a junior employee sitting in the **"Kamiza"** will be seen as **"Zuuzuushii"** (impudent or lacking sense).

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Psychology of "Safety and Respect"

Why is the seat furthest from the door the **Upper Seat**?

Historically, this was the safest position in case of an intruder from the outside. In modern business, it represents **"Ochitsuki"** (composure). The person sitting there is not distracted by the flow of people at the door.

When guiding **"Annai"**, you must follow the rule of **"Hidari Naname Mae"** (Diagonally forward to the left). Why the left side? Most people are right-handed. Walking on the left gives the customer open space on their right side, creating a feeling of being unconstrained.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Guiding to the Reception Room

**Situation:** You guide Mr. Smith (a customer) from the reception desk to the meeting room.

**An:** Mr. Smith, this way, please. **(Walking 2-3 steps ahead on the left)**
**An:** **There is a step here**, so please watch your footing.
**(In front of the meeting room door)**
**An:** **Excuse me.** **(Knocks 3 times, opens the door, and holds it)**
**An:** Please, come in.
**(Inside the meeting room)**
**An:** Please take the seat further inside. **(Pointing towards the Kamiza)**
**An:** Yamada will be with you shortly. Would you mind waiting for a moment?
**An:** **Excuse me.** **(Bows and gently closes the door)**

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Hidari Naname Mae"**: The ideal distance is 1-1.5 meters. Don't go too fast and leave the guest breathless, and don't go too slow and have the guest bump into your back.
2.  **"O-ashimoto ni go-chuui kudasai"**: This is the delicate concern characteristic of **Omotenashi**. Even if there isn't a high step, this reminder shows you are "caring" for the guest's safety.
3.  **"Oku no seki e"** (To the inner seat): Instead of saying "Sit here," you use "Please take the inner seat" to politely imply it is the Kamiza.
4.  **"Yamada ga mairimasu"**: Use the humble form for your internal colleague (Yamada) when speaking to a guest.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Complex "Kamiza" Puzzles on the BJT

The BJT doesn't just ask about simple meeting rooms. They present "tricky" scenarios:

- **Car Scenario**: Which position is the highest Kamiza if the driver is a company employee? Answer: **The back seat directly behind the driver**. But if the driver is a TAXI driver? The answer remains the same, but the #2 position shifts to behind the passenger seat.
- **Elevator Scenario**: If the elevator is very crowded, what should you do? BJT Answer: Apologize to the guest and stand as close to the control panel as possible to hold the button so the guest can exit first at the destination.
- **Restaurant Scenario**: What if the seat furthest from the door faces... the restroom? In this case, the seat with the **best view** or the **most formal position** (near a scroll or Tokonoma) becomes the Kamiza, regardless of the distance to the door. The BJT tests your flexibility.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Q1**: When guiding a guest up a staircase, where should you be?
A) Ahead of the guest.
B) Behind the guest.
C) Side-by-side.

**Q2**: In a meeting room with one long table and 4 chairs (2 on each side), the door is at a corner. Which seat is the "Shimoza" (lowest)?
A) The seat furthest from the door.
B) The seat closest to the door.

**Q3**: You are in an elevator with a guest. Reaching the destination floor, what should you do?
A) Door opens, you exit first to lead the way immediately.
B) Hold the "Open" button, invite the guest out first, then exit and lead.

> **Answers: Q1:B, Q2:B, Q3:B**
> **Deep Explanation**: On stairs, the employee should walk **BEHIND** the guest (to catch them if they fall—a safety consideration). In the elevator, your role is the "operator," ensuring the guest's smooth transition.
