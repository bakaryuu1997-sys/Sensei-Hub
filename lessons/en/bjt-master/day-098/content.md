## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The Truth Behind "We will consider it positively"

You are currently reading a reply email from a business partner. The response to your proposal for a new project is as follows:
**"Regarding this matter, we will consider it positively within our company."** (Honken ni tsukimashite wa, shanai de maemuki ni kentou sasete itadakimasu.)

Seeing this, do you rejoice, thinking, "Great, there's a high possibility it will be adopted!"?
If so, the road to BJT J1+ might still be long.

**Why do even N1-level learners get lost "between the lines"?**
Japanese business documents contain a unique **"Implicit Meaning" (Gengai no i)**.
- **"Consider positively" (Maemuki ni kentou suru)**: In reality, this often means, "(There is no reason to refuse for now, but I cannot promise to move forward actively) For the time being, I will keep it under advisement"—a polite postponement.
- **"Deal with appropriately" (Zensho shimasu)**: In many cases, it is used to mean, "(I cannot do more than this, but) I will maintain the appearance that I have done everything I could."

In Section 3, you are tested on your ability to accurately decipher the **"Context"** in which the document was sent and the **"True Intent"** of the sender, more than what is simply written as characters.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Section 3 - The "Excavation" of an Ancient City

Decoding documents in BJT Section 3 is like the **Excavation of an Ancient City**.

- **Ground Surface (Surface Words)**: These are pebbles and sand visible to anyone. Polite greetings and fixed phrases (e.g., "I appreciate your continued support") fall under this category.
- **Remains (Buried Logic)**: These are the foundations of buildings buried underground. This is the "true purpose" or "decision" of the document.

Amateurs are satisfied looking at the stones on the surface (fixed phrases), but J1+ elites see under which stone the important entrance lies and dig straight down to the deep layers. Section 3 is a battle against time. Instead of reading all words equally, you need the technique of narrowing down the **"Points to Excavate"** (conjunctions, closing words, nuances of negation).

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Elite Vocabulary Supporting Document "Logic"

### 1. Shift of Thought via "Conjunctions"
In J1+, grasping conjunctions that determine the flow of text is indispensable.
- **"Tsukimashite wa"**: A signal to enter the main subject (request or demand) based on the background so far.
- **"Saritote"**: "That being said" (adversative). A very formal and elegant way to explain a difficult situation.
- **"Hiite wa"**: "By extension," "consequently." Frequently used to describe long-term impacts.

### 2. Signs of "Euphemistic Rejection"
Rejection in documents avoids direct words and instead "hints" with expressions like:
- **"Ikan-nagara"**: "Regrettably." Contains a very strong sense of rejection.
- **"Itashikanemasu"**: "Cannot do." The most sophisticated form of "cannot."
- **"...wa hikae sasete itadakimasu"**: "We will refrain from doing... this time."

### 3. "Haishou" and "Sashuu"
- **"Haishou"**: A very stiff expression for "Understood/Acknowledged." Used in internal documents or formal replies.
- **"Go-sashuu kudasai"**: "Please check the contents and receive it." The highest business honorific for "Please look at this."

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Decoding Exercise for an Internal Email

**(The following is part of a reply email from a boss regarding a project delay report)**

> "I have **acknowledged (Haishou)** the circumstances regarding this matter.
> While the schedule delay is **regrettable (Ikan-nagara)**, I believe your judgment that forcing a push under the current system would risk **leading to (maneku)** a decline in quality is **appropriate (Shikarubeki)**.
> **Therefore (Tsukimashite wa)**, let us re-examine the matter, including the addition of personnel, at the beginning of next week."

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
1. **"Haishou"**: I have understood the situation (I might not be satisfied, but I have received the information).
2. **"Ikan-nagara"**: An expression of intent that he is very dissatisfied with the delay itself.
3. **"Shikarubeki"**: Natural, appropriate. He recognizes the subordinate's judgment (not to force it) itself.
4. **"Tsukimashite wa"**: From here begins the main subject (consultation on solutions).

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: "Skimming & Scanning" Strategy for Section 3

### ⚠️ Trap 1: Obsession with Greetings
Long greeting sentences starting with "Haikei" and continuing with "Toki-shita masu-masu...". Do not spend time here.
- **Strategy**: Scan greetings in 0.5 seconds and look for introductory words to the main subject like **"Sate," "Tsukimashite wa,"** or **"Kono tabi wa."**

### ⚠️ Trap 2: Disappearance of the Subject
In Japanese documents, the subject of "who does what" is often omitted.
- **Strategy**: Identify the actor from the form of Keigo (honorific or humble). If it is **"go-... itadaku,"** it is what the other party does; if it is **"o-... itasu,"** it is what you do.

### ⚠️ Trap 3: Interpretation of Polysemous Words
For example, **"Kentou"** (Consideration). Depending on the situation, it changes from a "positive consideration" to a "formal refusal."
- **Strategy**: Judge the "temperature" of the words from the overall tone of the email (whether there are many apologies, much gratitude, or strong demands).

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Section 3 Breakthrough - Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
A document arrived from a business partner saying, "Due to our company's regulations, we 'itashikanemasu' (cannot) cooperate in this matter." What is the correct meaning?
- A. We are in the middle of considering it now.
- B. We want to cooperate but don't know how.
- C. We are clearly refusing.
- D. We will cooperate after changing the regulations.

**Q2:**
An internal bulletin board says, "Please 'Go-sashuu' the budget proposals of each department and 'Request' (kou) a reply by the beginning of next week." What action should you take?
- A. Create a budget proposal and send it.
- B. Scrutinize the content of the sent budget proposals and reply.
- C. Throw the budget proposal in the trash.
- D. Go meet them directly without replying.

**Q3:**
What is the intent of the sentence: "This blunder could lead to a loss of our company's credibility, and 'by extension' (hiite wa) to a sense of distrust in the entire industry"?
- A. This problem is a problem for the whole industry, so the company is not responsible.
- B. It warns that the company's problem will have a further negative impact on a larger scope (the whole industry).
- C. It calls for a solution by cooperating as an entire industry.
- D. It says that the sense of distrust will disappear soon.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **C**: **"Itashikanemasu"** is a clear business refusal.
2. **B**: **"Go-sashuu"** means "to check the content carefully," and **"Kou"** means "to ask/request."
3. **B**: **"Hiite wa"** expresses how a cause ripples out further.
