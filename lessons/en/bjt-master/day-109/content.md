## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Rule the Exam, or Be Ruled by It

Tomorrow, what awaits you is a state of extremity for 100 minutes.
When aiming for J1+ in BJT (over 600 out of 800 points), the biggest enemy is not a "lack of vocabulary." It is the **"sense of impatience (**shousoukan**)"** regarding time and the **"paralysis of thought"** when faced with difficult questions.

Will you **persist (**koshitsu**)** too much in one question and miss the "earning spots" in the second half?
Or will you **discard (**kikyaku**)** uncertain information and move on to the next question with detachment?

The victory or defeat of an elite is decided by the **"bird's-eye view (**fukan**)"** immediately after the start of the exam. Today, I will teach you the final mental tactics to turn your knowledge into "score."

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Final Sprint - "A Marathon with a Time Bomb"

The BJT exam is like a **"Marathon with a Time Bomb on Your Back."**

- **Pace Allocation**: Just running fast will make you run out of breath. You need to run through flat roads (easy questions) at full speed, purposefully slow down on uphill slopes (difficult questions), and reach the goal before the bomb explodes.
- **Discarding Luggage**: It's impossible to finish the race while holding onto luggage that's too heavy (a question you can't solve). Sometimes, the courage to say "I'll discard this question" is the only key to finishing.

You are the runner and, at the same time, the "coach" who manages your own condition.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Vocabulary for Maintaining Mental "Equilibrium"

### 1. Letting Go of Obsession and Regaining Calmness
- **"Koshitsu suru"**: To cling to one idea or thing and not let go (Persistence/Obsession). In an exam, "obsession with one question" becomes fatal.
- **"Kikyaku suru"**: To discard, reject, or dismiss. Discard uncertain inferences and concentrate only on facts.
- **"Fukan suru"**: To look down at the whole from a high place (Bird's-eye view).

### 2. Expressing Pressure
- **"Shousou-kan"**: A sense of impatience/fretfulness.
- **"An-un ga tachikomeru"**: To have a bad premonition; a sign that the situation is getting worse (Dark clouds gathering).
- **"Fusshoku suru"**: To wipe away bad feelings or dirt completely. "Wipe away (Fusshoku) anxiety."

### 3. The Best State
- **"Taizen-jijaku"**: Being composed and undaunted no matter what happens.
- **"Entering the Zone"**: A state of extreme concentration where surrounding sounds disappear and performance is maximized.

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- **"Taizen-jijaku"**: The mental state most required of an executive.
- **"Onozu-to"**: Naturally; of its own accord.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The "80/20" Rule to Pull J1+ Toward You

### ⚠️ Strategy 1: The 15-Second "Abandonment" (Mikiri)
In Section 3 (Reading) of BJT, if you cannot find the "grounds for the answer" within 15 seconds after reading the question, that question is a candidate for a **"Discard Question" (Sute-mon)**. Mark it randomly and move on to save time.

### ⚠️ Strategy 2: "Overtaking" the Audio
In Sections 1 and 2, it is an absolute condition to finish reading the options before the audio. Reading while listening is J2 level. J1+ is the stance of **"finishing reading and then waiting for the answer to flow in."**

### ⚠️ Strategy 3: The "Shutter" That Doesn't Drag Out Mistakes
Even if you think you made a mistake on the previous question, lower an **"Iron Shutter"** in your head the moment the next question starts. Only those who can discard the past can grasp the scores of the future.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Day 109 - Mentality and Tactics Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
During the exam, you encountered a difficult question that you absolutely cannot solve. What action describes "not **persisting** in one question and making a **bird's-eye** judgment"?
- A. Continued thinking for 30 minutes until the question was solved.
- B. Ran out of the exam venue while crying.
- C. Considered the time allocation, postponed the question or marked it randomly, and concentrated on other solvable questions.
- D. Peeked at the neighbor's answer.

**Q2:**
What is an appropriate preparation to "**wipe away (Fusshoku)** anxiety and face the real thing with **composure (Taizen-jijaku)**"?
- A. Stay up all night and memorize 1,000 new words.
- B. Review the questions solved so far, solidify your success image, and get enough sleep.
- C. Become desperate, thinking the exam doesn't matter.
- D. Drink with friends until morning.

**Q3:**
What is the correct usage of the word **"Kikyaku"** (Discard/Reject)?
- A. We discarded a wonderful proposal and adopted it.
- B. We discarded (Kikyaku) groundless rumors and confirmed the facts.
- C. We discarded a present from a friend and were happy.
- D. Discarded every day and became healthy.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **C**: Professional tactics involve avoiding **"Koshitsu" (clinging)** and taking a **"Fukan" (overall view)**.
2. **B**: Conditioning of mind and body is indispensable for **"Fusshoku" (wiping away)** and **"Taizen-jijaku" (composure)**.
3. **B**: **"Kikyaku"** is used when rejecting unnecessary or incorrect things.
