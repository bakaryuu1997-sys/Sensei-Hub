# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 12
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Meetings and Briefings [Shanai Kaigi - Uchiai] - The Etiquette of Expressing Opinions and Consensus Building
### 📊 Target Level: J3 -> J2+ (Professional Discussion)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The "Invisible Presence" in the Meeting Room

In an internal meeting at a Japanese company, you sit in silence from beginning to end, nodding vigorously every time your boss speaks. You think: "I am showing absolute respect and listening intently."

The Reality: You are being evaluated as **「主体性がない」 (Shutaisei ga nai - Lacking initiative/agency)**.

Japanese people value **「和」 (Wa - Harmony)** extremely highly, but "Harmony" does not mean silence. In the BJT exam, especially at the J1+ level, they don't just test whether you understand the meeting content, but also whether you know how to **「意見を求める」 (Ask for opinions)** or **「異議を唱える」 (Propose a counter-argument)** in a sophisticated way without ruining the atmosphere (**Kuuki**). If you only know how to say **「賛成です」** (I agree), you will forever remain at the "textbook N1" level.

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Collective Intelligence "Yose-nabe" (Hot Pot)

A Japanese corporate meeting is like cooking a **Yose-nabe** (a mixed hot pot).
- Each member is an ingredient (Shrimp, fish, vegetables, mushrooms).
- The facilitator is the one in charge of the flavor.

For the pot to be delicious, everyone must "contribute" their ingredients at the right time. If you are a "Mushroom" but stay silent in the basket, the pot will lack depth. However, if you are a "Chili" and jump in too early or too harshly, you will ruin the whole pot for everyone.
The art of meetings is knowing when to "jump into the pot" and how to let your flavor harmonize with others to create the most balanced **「結論」 (Conclusion)**.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The "Hidden Mechanism" of Consensus

Why do meetings in Japan sometimes go very quickly, and it seems like everyone has agreed beforehand? That is thanks to **「根回し」 (Nemawashi)**.

1. **Nemawashi**: Literally means digging around the roots of a tree before relocating it. In business, it refers to private, informal consultations and getting unofficial agreement before the official meeting takes place. If you bring up a shocking opinion in the meeting without having done "Nemawashi" with your boss, you will be seen as lacking social skills.
2. **Kuuki wo yomu (Reading the Air)**: In meetings, sometimes a boss asks, "Does anyone have an opinion?" but their tone of voice implies, "Don't anyone object." A J1+ individual must hear that "Tone" and adjust their speech accordingly.
3. **Tatamikakeru**: A technique of delivering arguments in rapid succession when you see the opponent is wavering, in order to close the deal/issue.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Considering a New Project

**Situation:** An and Boss Yamada along with colleague Sato are meeting about accelerating the progress of Project X.

**Manager Yamada:** Now, regarding Project X, I am thinking of completing the prototype by next month. Please let me hear your opinions.
**Sato:** By next month? With our current resources, I think it might be a bit difficult.
**An:** **Mr. Sato's concerns are perfectly valid, but** I believe that accelerating the schedule is **possible**.
**Manager Yamada:** Ho, and why do you think so, Ms. An?
**An:** Yes. By **proceeding with processes A and B in parallel**, we can expect a reduction of about one week. Also, if we can get cooperation from external partners, further speed-up is expected.
**Sato:** I see. However, concerns remain regarding quality control.
**An:** **You are absolutely right**. Therefore, regarding the inspection process, we need to perform **Nemawashi** with the specialist team in advance to set up a system for smooth coordination.
**Manager Yamada:** Very well, then could you please rework the specific plan in that direction?
**An:** **Understood**. I will get to work on it immediately.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Go-kenen mo mottomo desu ga"** (Your concerns are valid, but...): A great way to "cushion" before contradicting a colleague. It means "Your worry is completely reasonable, BUT...". This prevents the relationship from becoming tense.
2.  **"~ka to zonjimasu ga"**: A softening expression instead of asserting "It's impossible" (Muri desu). In business, excessive assertion is often seen as lacking humility.
3.  **"Heikou shite susumeru"** (Proceeding in parallel): Using professional terminology adds weight and high persuasiveness to your opinion (Logical thinking).
4.  **"Nemawashi wo shite oki"**: Your use of this term shows you deeply understand the operational procedures of Japanese companies, creating trust with the boss.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Decoding BJT Section 2 (Discussion)

In the BJT discussion section, you will often encounter questions like: **「この後、安さんは何をしますか？」** (What will An do after this?).

- **Logic 1: Next Action.** The answer usually lies in the facilitator's last statement or the employee's last confirmation. For example: **「練り直してくれないか」** (Could you rework it?) -> The action is **「再検討」 (Reconsideration/Rework)**.
- **Logic 2: Distinguishing Opinion vs. Fact.** BJT will trick you by presenting information as a "Fact" (Data) and asking if the speaker "Agrees" (Opinion) with it. Pay attention to sentence endings like **「～ではないでしょうか」** (Is it not...?)—this is an extremely common way to offer an opinion.
- **Logic 3: Roles.** Identify the final decision-maker. In a Japanese company, no matter how lively the discussion, the final conclusion belongs to the person with the highest rank.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: In a meeting, when you want to interrupt someone to add an opinion, which is the most polite?
- A) Wait a moment.
- B) Let me speak.
- C) Excuse me for interrupting your talk. May I add one point?
- D) That is wrong.

**Question 2**: When your boss proposes a plan that you see as high-risk, what should you say?
- A) I think that way is bad.
- B) You are absolutely right, but I have a few concerns regarding the cost.
- C) It has nothing to do with me.

**Question 3**: After the meeting ends, what is the most important thing an "Elite" employee should do?
- A) Go to lunch immediately.
- B) Write the **「議事録」 (Meeting Minutes)** and send it to members to reconfirm the agreed-upon items.
- C) Forget everything that was said.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: C**. Standard for intervening politely.
> - **Q2: B**. Always start with consensus/acknowledgment before pointing out risks.
> - **Q3: B**. Minutes are the most important "weapon" to avoid "he-said-she-said" trouble.
