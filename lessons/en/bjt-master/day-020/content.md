# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 20
## 🎯 Theme: Schedule Adjustment & Management [Schedule no Chousei to Kanri] - Advanced Etiquette for Appointments and Rescheduling
### 📊 Target Level: J2 -> J1+ (Time Management & Coordination Mastery)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: "On Time" is "Late"

In Japanese business culture, there is an implicit rule called **「5分前行動」 (Gofun-mae koudou - The 5-minute rule)**.
If a meeting starts at 10:00, and you walk into the room exactly at 10:00, you might be considered **「遅刻」 (Chikoku - Late)**. Why? Because 10:00 is the time to **Start Working**, not the time to prepare your seat, open your computer, or get your notebook out.

Furthermore, imagine you have overlapping appointments. You message your boss: **「別の予定が入ったので、明日の会議を来週に変えてください」** [Because I have another appointment, please change tomorrow's meeting to next week].

Immediately, you are labeled as **「仕事ができない人」 (Shigoto ga dekinai hito - Someone who can't get the job done)**. The reason isn't the rescheduling itself, but your failure to **「打診」 (Dashin - Probe/Consult)**—effectively ordering your boss instead of asking—and your lack of priority management (**Yuusen jun'i**).

At the J1+ level, schedule management is not just taking notes in a diary; it's the art of coordinating your time with others' time in the most respectful way possible.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Air Traffic Controller

Managing schedules in a Japanese company is like the job of an **Air Traffic Controller**.
- Each project and meeting is an airplane flying in the sky.
- The destination is the company's success.
- **An N1 level member**: Is a pilot who only knows how to fly their own plane. When they see fog (an issue), they frantically shout for an emergency landing without caring about the other planes in the queue.
- **A J1+ level member**: Is the person in the control tower. They see the entire sky.
    - They know which plane is "Running low on fuel" (more urgent) to prioritize its landing.
    - They arrange flights so that no plane has to circle for too long (wasting time).
    - They always have a "Backup plan" if the weather changes.

When you manage your schedule well, every "flight" of work will land safely and on time, without causing any disruption to the system.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The "Religion" of Time

In Japan, time is not just money; it is **「信用」 (Shin'you - Credibility/Trust)**.

1. **Strict Punctuality (Jikan no genshu)**: Absolute adherence to time. Even a 1-minute delay is late. If you predict you'll be late by even 1 minute, you must call to inform them immediately.
2. **Avoiding Double Booking**: Allowing schedules to overlap is a disgrace to your professional competence. It shows you lack control over your own work.
3. **Presenting Candidate Dates (Kouhobi no teiji)**: When proposing a meeting schedule, always offer **3 options**. Never ask, "When are you free?". Ask: **「以下の3つの候補日のうち、ご都合の良い時間はございますか」** [Among the following 3 candidate times, is there a time that is convenient for you?]. This is how you help the other party save time thinking.
4. **Provisional Booking (Kari-osae)**: "Temporary hold." J1+ always knows how to request a temporary hold on a time slot while waiting for confirmation from a third party, avoiding gaps in the schedule.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Rescheduling an Appointment

**Situation:** An has scheduled a meeting with Manager Yamada for tomorrow afternoon. However, a VIP customer has just requested an urgent meeting at that exact time. An must persuade her boss to reschedule.

**An:** Manager Yamada, I am very sorry. I have something I would like to **discuss** regarding tomorrow's 3:00 PM meeting.
**Yamada:** Tomorrow's meeting? Did something happen?
**An:** Yes. Actually, I just received a call from Mr. Tanaka of Company A, and he will be visiting us urgently at 3:00 PM tomorrow. It's for the **confirmation of important matters** regarding the new project, so it's a case I cannot miss.
**Yamada:** I see. If it's Company A, it can't be helped.
**An:** I am very sorry for the inconvenience, but **could I possibly reschedule** my meeting with you to another day?
**Yamada:** Hmm, my schedule is quite full this week.
**An:** I understand. **Taking your convenience into account**, I have listed three alternative candidate dates.
1. Wednesday the 18th, after 11:00 AM
2. Thursday the 19th, after 1:00 PM
3. Friday the 20th, all day
If there is a time that works for you among these, I will adjust accordingly.
**Yamada:** Well, I'm free from 2:00 PM on the 19th.
**An:** Thank you very much! Then, **I will change it to 2:00 PM on the 19th**. I apologize once again for the inconvenience caused.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Go-soudan"** (Discussion/Consultation): Never say "I want to change." Use "I'd like to discuss/get your advice on changing." It sounds much more humble and cooperative.
2.  **"Hazurenai anken"** (A matter that cannot be missed): A strong phrase to emphasize the importance of the reason. The boss will understand this is not a frivolous change.
3.  **"Kouhobi wo mittsu hodo"** (About 3 candidate dates): The golden rule of J1+. Providing options allows the boss to simply "decide" instead of having to search their own calendar.
4.  **"Go-tsugou wo kouryo shi"** (Taking your convenience into account): This shows you have already looked at the boss's general schedule before asking, rather than speaking thoughtlessly.
5.  **"Kasunete owabi moushiagemasu"** (I apologize once again): Apologizing again at the end of the conversation to smooth over any annoyance you may have caused the boss.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: "Schedule" Problems in BJT Listening & Reading

In the BJT, time-related data is always the most sophisticated "trap."

- **Trap 1: Constant changes.** In a long dialogue, a meeting schedule might be changed 2-3 times (e.g., "Monday" -> "No, I'm busy Tuesday" -> "How about Wednesday afternoon?"). The question always asks for the **Finalized Schedule**.
- **Trap 2: Time terminology.** BJT often uses words like **「下旬」 (Gechun - Late in the month)**, **「翌週」 (Yokushuu - Next week)**, and **「隔週」 (Kakushuu - Every other week)**. If you don't know these terms, you'll pick the wrong date on the calendar.
- **Trap 3: Priority.** BJT Section 3 often presents 3 tasks arising simultaneously and asks which the employee should do first. The correct answer is always based on: **Urgency (Deadline)** and **Partner Importance (Client > Internal)**.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: You want to schedule a short 15-minute meeting with your boss. Which request is the most "Elite"?
- A) Are you free now?
- B) Excuse me, do you have time?
- C) **"Manager, could I have about 15 minutes of your time this afternoon? It's for a report on the new project."**
- D) Let's have a meeting tomorrow.

**Question 2**: When you discover a double booking between two internal meetings. Which meeting should you prioritize?
- A) The meeting with your direct boss attending.
- B) The meeting with your closest colleague.
- C) **The meeting regarding a current issue (Troubleshooting).**
- D) The meeting taking place closest to your desk.

**Question 3**: When should the "Re-confirmation" (Remind) of a schedule be performed?
- A) 1 minute before the meeting starts.
- B) **"Send a reminder email on the evening of the day before or the morning of that day."**
- C) Not necessary if the boss promised.
- D) After the meeting has finished.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: C**. Option C includes everything: the target (**Manager**), the estimated time (**15 min**), and most importantly, the **Content**.
> - **Q2: C**. In business, **Urgency** is the most important criterion. An ongoing issue must be resolved immediately to avoid major damage.
> - **Q3: B**. This is a **"Remind"** action that shows extremely high professionalism. It helps the busy boss prepare and shows you value the meeting.
