## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Giving "Instructions," Not "Requests"

An emergency has occurred. The system is down, and every second counts. As a leader, you must give precise instructions to your members.
**"I'm sorry, but could you possibly take a look at the system for a bit?"** (Sumimasen ga, chotto shisutemu wo miteoite morayemasu ka?)

In this case, the priority of the instructions, the deadline for completion, and the location of responsibility are all unclear. BJT Section 1 tests command expressions accompanied by **"Clarity"** and **"Dignity"** to move an organization, beyond mere "Politeness."

**Why do even N1-level learners miss "Commands"?**
Because sophisticated instructions in Japanese often take a form that looks like a "request" or "hope" at first glance.
- **"...saretai"**: This is not a desire of "I want to," but a strong "instruction" (shall/should).
- **"Irou no nai you"**: This is a command to "Ensure there are no omissions/oversights."
Mistaking these for "soft requests" can lead to fatal business mistakes.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Command & Instructions - "Programming a Computer"

Instructions by a leader are like **Programming a Computer**.

- **Ambiguous Code (Inappropriate Instructions)**: The computer will encounter errors (misunderstandings/mistakes) and stop. Words like "yoshinari" (as you see fit) or "tekitou ni" (properly/appropriately) are breeding grounds for bugs.
- **Accurate Code (J1+ Instructions)**: You clarify the arguments (deadline, person in charge, method) and define even the execution results (reports).

Listening to "commands" in BJT is the work of deciphering the **"algorithm"** of who the audio is trying to make do what, by when, and with what priority.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: "Assertive" (Danteiteki) Vocabulary for Moving Organizations

### 1. "...saretai" and "...saretashi" Expressing Strong Instructions
Used in official documents, high-level business documents, or top-down instructions.
- **"Sumiyaka ni houkoku saretai"**: Report promptly (a strong command).
- **"Banzen wo kisaretai"**: Prepare perfectly (ensure perfection).

### 2. "Irou no nai you" (Leaving no stone unturned)
A fixed phrase in situations where mistakes are not allowed.
- **"Saibu ni itaru made irou no nai you chuui saretai"** (Please be careful so that there are no omissions, down to the details).
- **"Chikuichi houkoku suru koto"**: Report each and every thing in detail.

### 3. "Soukou suru" (To prove successful)
Used when wishing for or being convinced that one's instructions or measures will go well.
- **"Kono tabi no sochi ga soukou suru koto wo kitai suru"** (I expect that this measure will prove successful).

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE: The "Command Center" During a System Failure

### Scene: Midnight at a data center, instructing recovery work
- **Leader**: "Everyone, please **pay close attention (Keichuu saretai)**. Currently, the main server is down. Team A, identify the cause immediately and report **each and every detail (Chikuichi)** within 30 minutes."
- **Member**: "Understood. We will start immediately."
- **Leader**: "Team B, **ensure perfection (Banzen wo kisaretai)** in the preparation for switching to backup. To minimize the impact on customers, **ensure there are no omissions (Irou no nai you)**. I hope that this prompt response will **prove successful (Soukou suru)** for the resumption of service tomorrow morning. That is all."

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- **"Keichuu saretai"**: Devote your attention (listen well).
- **"Banzen wo kisu"**: To make perfect preparations so that there is not even a slight mistake.
- **"Todomeru"**: To keep within that range/limit.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Distinguishing the "Strength of Instructions" in Section 1

Criteria for identifying command/instruction questions in BJT Section 1:

### ⚠️ Point 1: "Degree of Command" at the End of the Sentence
- **Lv. 1 "...te kudasai"**: Standard request.
- **Lv. 2 "...te itadakitai"**: Polite but strong desire (substantial instruction).
- **Lv. 3 "...saretai"**: Clear duty/official order.
- **Lv. 4 "...suru koto"**: Absolute rule, as in manuals or notices.

### ⚠️ Point 2: "Degree of Urgency" by Adverbs
- **"Sumiyaka ni"**: Promptly.
- **"Tadachi ni"**: Immediately (higher urgency than 'sumiyaka ni').
- **"Otte"**: Later/Follow-up.

### ⚠️ Point 3: Negative Commands
- **"...wa hikaeru you ni"**: Refrain from... (Don't do...).
- **"...wa genkin to suru"**: Strictly prohibited.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Day 104 - Command & Instructions Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
A company-wide email from the CEO says, "Regarding the progress of the new project, please 'Chikuichi' (in detail) report it by every Friday 'Saretai' (should/command)." What is the correct understanding?
- A. The CEO wants you to report whenever you feel like it.
- B. The CEO orders that detailed contents must be reported by every Friday.
- C. The CEO only "wishes" to have reports made, but it's not mandatory.
- D. Friday is a holiday, so you don't have to report.

**Q2:**
Which expression has the same meaning as "Irou no nai you"?
- A. So that there are no omissions.
- B. Including an apology.
- C. Looking forward to it.
- D. Unluckily.

**Q3:**
It was said, "This strategy **'Soukou'** (proved successful)." What was the result?
- A. It was a big failure.
- B. It had the expected effect.
- C. It was meaningless.
- D. It hasn't started yet.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **B**: **"...saretai"** is a strong command expression, and **"Chikuichi"** means in detail.
2. **A**: **"Irou"** means "omission/oversight."
3. **B**: **"Soukou"** means to perform/produce a feat, i.e., to achieve an effect.
