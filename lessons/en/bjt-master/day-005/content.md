## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Arriving "Too Early" (The 5-Minute Rule)

Imagine you have an important meeting with a partner at **14:00**. Wanting to show the enthusiasm and seriousness of an N1 holder, you arrive at the building lobby at **13:50** and head straight to the reception desk at **13:53**. You smile confidently: **"14-ji ni o-yakusoku shite orimasu Yamada desu"**.

To your surprise, after you enter the meeting room, the person in charge from the partner's side walks in looking slightly rushed, adjusting their tie while saying: **"O-matase shite moushiwake arimasen"** (Sorry to have kept you waiting).

**Why were you given a silent "minus point"?**
In Japanese business culture (and BJT logic), arriving **too early** (more than 5-7 minutes before) is considered a lack of consideration (**Haigyo busoku**). Why? Because you are forcing the other party to speed up their current work to accommodate you.

The BJT tests not just your punctuality, but also whether you know how to "disappear" gracefully until the golden moment.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Visiting - "Intrusion" into the Partner's Domain

Think of visiting a partner's company as **Requesting a Resource** from another Server.
- **Reception Desk (Uketsuke)**: This is the Firewall. You must provide the correct authentication (Name, Company, Purpose) to be granted Access.
- **Waiting Room (Ousetsu-shitsu)**: This is the Buffer zone. You have entered the system but are not yet permitted to execute commands.
- **The Wait**: This is the data Sync time.

If you arrive too early, you are creating a **High Priority Thread** that forces the partner's server to interrupt other tasks. A J1+ expert knows how to "Idle" at a nearby cafe or the public lobby until exactly **13:55** (5 minutes before the appointment) before sending the formal Access request.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Psychology of Reception and the Awareness of "Shimoza"

### 1. "Toritsugi" - The Art of Relaying
When you stand before the reception desk, you are not talking to an individual; you are talking to a **Channel**. Your words must be extremely concise yet complete so the receptionist can relay them accurately via the internal phone (**Naisen**).
- Standard Structure: **[Company Name] + [Your Name] + [Appointment Time] + [Name of person you are meeting]**.

### 2. "Silence" While Waiting - Power in Stillness
When led into a meeting room and asked to **"Shoushou omachi kudasai"** (Please wait a moment):
- Many N1 holders take the opportunity to check their phones or walk around looking at the paintings on the wall. **Wrong!**
- **Under the hood**: The "invisible observation" of the partner company's staff is always present. The correct action is to sit up straight at the **Shimoza (Lower Seat)** (the seat closest to the door), place your bag neatly at your feet, and maintain absolute stillness. This is a sign of your **"Kakugo"** (resolve) and readiness to work seriously.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: From Reception to Entering the Room

### Scene 1: Handling the Reception Desk
- **Yamada:** Osore-irimasu. 14-ji ni Tanaka-buchou to o-yakusoku wo itadaite orimasu, ABC Co., Ltd. no Yamada to mōshimasu.
- **Reception:** ABC Co., Ltd. no Yamada-sama de irasshaimasu ne. Shōchi itashimashita. Tadaima kakunin itashimasu no de, achira no sofa de omachi itadakemasu deshō ka.

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- Yamada uses the phrase **"o-yakusoku wo itadaite orimasu"** instead of `yakusoku shimashita` to show gratitude for the partner's time.
- The receptionist uses **"de irasshaimasu ne"** (High-level honorific) to begin the process of welcoming an "outside" (Soto) guest.

### Scene 2: Being Guided and Entering the Room
- **Guide:** O-matase itashimashita. Kochira e douzo.
- (In front of the meeting room door)
- **Guide:** Kochira no heya de omachi kudasai. Tanaka wa mamonaku mairimasu.
- **Yamada:** Arigatou gozaimasu. Shitsurei itashimasu.

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- When entering the room, even if it is empty, Yamada must say **"Shitsurei itashimasu"** as a ritual of asking permission to step into the partner's space.
- Note: The guide refers to their boss as **"Tanaka"** (no title/honorific) because to Yamada, the boss is "inside" (Uchi) for the guide. This is consistent with Day 3 logic.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Decoding J1+ Level Traps

### ⚠️ Trap 1: Timing of Removing Your Coat
Frequent in BJT winter scenarios:
- **Question**: Where should you remove your coat?
- **Elite Logic**: You must remove your coat **before entering the building** or **before reaching the reception desk**. Never stand in front of the receptionist while struggling to take off your coat. Removing it beforehand shows you are ready to shake off the "dust" of the outside world and enter the partner's clean space.

### ⚠️ Trap 2: Timing of Sitting Down
After being led into the meeting room:
- **The Trap**: The guide says "Douzo" and you sit down immediately.
- **Elite Logic**: You should stand and wait until the person in charge (your appointment) appears and sit down together after greetings. If they haven't arrived, you may sit, but you must choose the **Shimoza (Lower Seat)** - the seat closest to the door. If you accidentally sit in the **Kamiza (Upper Seat)** (the seat furthest from the door, usually with a view), you will be judged as arrogant.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Elite Practice Challenge

**Question 1:**
You visit a partner, but the person you have an appointment with (Tanaka) is suddenly busy and sends another employee to meet you. Which response is the most Elite?
- A. Tanaka-san wa doko desu ka?
- B. O-isogashii tokoro kyoukyuku desu ga, kawari ni okoshi itadaki arigatou gozaimasu.
- C. Tanaka-san ni aenakute zannen desu.
- D. Shōchi itashimashita. De wa, o-namae wo ukagatte mo yoroshii deshō ka.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: B**. J1+ proficiency requires empathy for the partner's situation. Thanking the substitute (**kawari ni okoshi itadaki**) shows respect for the entire organization's time.

**Question 2:**
When there is no one at the reception desk, only an internal phone and a list of extension numbers, what should you do?
- A. Wait until someone passes by.
- B. Enter the office on your own to find someone.
- C. Pick up the phone, dial the correct extension for the person you're meeting, and introduce yourself.
- D. Give up and go home, then send an email complaining.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: C**. The BJT tests **Self-reliance** in business processes. Calling the extension with a structure similar to the reception desk is the standard procedure.

**Question 3:**
In the waiting room, where should you place your business bag to avoid an "unconscious" error?
- A. On the meeting table for easy access to documents.
- B. On the empty chair next to you.
- C. On the floor, right next to your own feet.
- D. Keep it on your shoulder even while sitting.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: C**. Tables and chairs are for humans or important documents. Placing a bag (which touched the ground outside) on them is considered dirtying the space. Placing it at your feet is the most humble approach.
