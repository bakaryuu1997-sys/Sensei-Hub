## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: "Keep Going" When You Can't Say "Keep Going"

A subordinate lost in a competition for a project they poured their heart and soul (**shinketsu**) into. Or, the person in charge at a business partner has fallen ill and been hospitalized.
What do you say to them?
**"That's too bad. Please do your best next time."** (Zannen deshita ne. Tsugi wa ganbatte kudasai.)

These words are by no means wrong. However, for a leader aiming for J1+, one must say they are far too "thin."
On the battlefield of business, true empathy is to accurately **"conjecture" (Haisatsu)** the other party's pain or fatigue and quietly push them from behind without hurting their pride. In BJT Section 1, your delicate communication ability to decipher such **"Subtleties of the Heart" (Kokoro no kibi)** is tested.

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Encouragement & Empathy - "Resonance of a Tuning Fork"

Empathy in business is like the **Resonance of a Tuning Fork**.

- **Mismatch (Shallow Empathy)**: If the other party is making a low, heavy sound (discouragement) and you make a high sound (carefree encouragement), dissonance occurs, and the other party will close their heart.
- **Resonance (J1+ Empathy)**: First, you vibrate at the same frequency as the other party. By staying close and saying, "I can imagine (osasshi-shimasu) your painful situation," the door to the heart opens for the first time. Then, by gradually changing your own vibration to a positive frequency, you naturally lift the other party up.

Instead of forcibly pulling the other party up, you **"appreciate their labor" (negirau)** from the same eye level. This process of resonance is the magic that unifies an organization.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Vocabulary of "Compassion" Supporting Mental Support

### 1. "Haisatsu" (Conjecture) to Sympathize with the Other's Heartache
The highest-level Keigo for "to guess/sense."
- **"Goshintsuu no hodo, osasshi-itashimasu"** (I sympathize with the depth of your heartache).
- **"Haisatsu-suru ni, soutou na gokurou ga atta koto to zonjimasu"** (Inferring from the situation, I believe there was considerable hardship).

### 2. "Kendo-chourai" for Those Who Failed
A sophisticated four-character idiom (Yojijukugo) referring to someone who has been defeated once returning with renewed vigor and momentum.
- **"This time was regrettable, but let's aim for 'Kendo-chourai' (a comeback)."**
- **"O-chikara-otoshi no nai you"**: A kind consideration meaning "Please don't be too discouraged."

### 3. "Itami-irimasu" for Gratitude for Kindness
A word to convey trepidation and gratitude when the other party worries about you or provides a convenience for you.
- **"Go-teinei na gekirei wo itadaki, itami-irimasu"** (I am deeply moved/obliged by your polite encouragement).

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE: "The Office at Night" After a Competition Loss

### Scene: Boss and subordinate after giving their all but losing the project to another company
- **Subordinate**: "I'm sorry. After all that preparation, due to my lack of ability..."
- **Boss**: "No, I am **fully aware** (× shitte-iru → shouchi-shite-iru) of your efforts so far. This proposal won technically. **Please don't be too discouraged (O-chikara-otoshi no nai you).**"
- **Subordinate**: "Hearing you say that... but still, it's frustrating."
- **Boss**: "**Gashin-shoutan** (Enduring hardship for the goal)—now is the time to use that frustration as a spring. I truly expect your comeback and a **Kendo-chourai** next time. Go home early today and **appreciate (negirai)** your body."

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- **"Gashin-shoutan"**: To endure hardship for a long time in order to achieve a goal.
- **"Negirau"**: To appreciate someone's labor, to thank. Used especially from a superior to a subordinate (or among equals).

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Measuring the "Temperature of Empathy" in Section 1

攻略 points for when encouragement/empathy questions appear in BJT Section 1:

### ⚠️ Point 1: Confirmation of the Superior-Subordinate Relationship
"Negirau" (to appreciate labor) is, in principle, used from a **superior to a subordinate** (or a colleague). Conversely, it may be rude for a subordinate to "negirau" a boss's hardship.
- **Strategy**: For the case of subordinate to boss, judge whether forms like **"Otsukaresama de gozaimasu"** or **"Itami-irimasu"** are appropriate.

### ⚠️ Point 2: "Prayer" Instead of "Prohibition"
In BJT, the form of consideration **"O-chikara-otoshi no nai you (I pray)"** is preferred over a negative form like "Don't be depressed."

### ⚠️ Point 3: Nuance of Four-Character Idioms
Choices containing four-character idioms like "Kendo-chourai" or "Gashin-shoutan" often reflect an extremely high J1+-level professional consciousness and are strong candidates.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Day 103 - Encouragement & Empathy Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
What is the most appropriate phrase for a colleague who is depressed after a big failure?
- A. Aiming for 'Kendo-chourai,' let's do our best again from tomorrow.
- B. It's not my fault, so don't be discouraged.
- C. Haishou-itashimashita. Please be careful next time.
- D. Although I appreciate it, let's give up this time.

**Q2:**
Which phrase is appropriate when a business partner returns from illness?
- A. O-karada wo o-itawari kudasai. (Please take care of/be kind to your body.)
- B. O-karada wo haisatsu-itashimasu.
- C. O-karada wo songiri-shite kudasai. (Please 'stop-loss' your body.)
- D. Ainiku no o-karada desu ne. (What an unfortunate body.)

**Q3:**
What is the speaker's situation when they say, "I am 'Itami-iru' (obliged) by your kind response"?
- A. They are being yelled at by the other party.
- B. They are so grateful for the other party's kindness that they feel sorry.
- C. They are pointing out the other party's mistake.
- D. They are simply saying they are in pain.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **A**: **"Kendo-chourai"** is a very positive encouragement prompting a comeback.
2. **A**: **"Itawaru"** is a word to care for illness or fatigue.
3. **B**: **"Itami-iru"** is a humble gratitude expressing that the other party's kindness is more than one deserves.
