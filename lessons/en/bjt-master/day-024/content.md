# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 24
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Procedures and Requests - Precise and Smooth Coordination with Admin/HR
### 📊 Target Level: J3 -> J2+ (Administrative Coordination Mastery)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The "Forgotten" Request

You need a new parking card key, so you head to the General Affairs (Soumu) desk. You say:
**"Excuse me, I rented a parking spot, so please give me a card."**

The clerk responds:
**"Please submit an application form."**
You reply, surprised: "An application? I'm in a hurry, can't you just give it to me now?"

The result: Not only did you not get the card immediately, but you've been labeled as a **"loose person" (loose on rules)** by the Admin staff.
In a Japanese company, back-office departments (HR, Admin, IT) are not your "servants." They are professionals in **"Management" (Kanri)**, ensuring the company operates correctly. If you ignore rules (procedures) and approach them with a lack of consideration, your requests will be moved to the bottom of the priority list. A J1+ level professional knows that the fastest way to get their own work done is to first "make the other person's job as easy as possible."

---

## 🎭 2. THE ANALOGY: The Watch Movement and the Support Gear

Imagine the company as a giant **mechanical watch**.
- Sales or Manufacturing are the **"Hands"** on the outside (directly generating revenue).
- HR, Admin, and IT are the **"Gears"** turning on the inside (supporting employee life and tools).

No matter how beautiful the hands are, if the internal gears get jammed or run out of oil, the watch stops.
- **The N1-Level Employee**: Thinks they are the "Hand," so they have the right to command the "Gear."
- **The J1+ Level Master**: Understands that every gear has its own **"Engagement" (Procedure)**. You cannot force a gear to turn by hand. You must align yourself with that engagement and apply the right lubricant (a polite attitude) to keep the whole system running smoothly.

When you follow the procedure, you are helping the "Admin Gear" turn faster, which results in your own requests being resolved with divine speed.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: "Etiquette" Toward Internal Members

In Japanese companies, the distinction between **"Miuichi" (Internal members)** and **"External"** is clear, but even toward internal members, **"Etiquette" (Reigi)** is required when making requests to other departments.

1. **Compliance with Rules**: Always start by confirming the process: "What kind of procedures are necessary?" Do not ask for arbitrary exceptions (shortcuts) unless it is a genuine emergency.
2. **Adherence to Deadlines**: If Admin asks you to submit something by the 10th, a J1+ professional submits it by the 8th. Submitting at the last minute is considered **"annoying behavior" (Meiwaku-koui)**.
3. **Respect Professional Expertise**: Never think of Admin work as "chores." They are experts in legal compliance, personnel, and asset management. Phrases like **"I apologize for the intrusion during your busy time, but could I ask for your assistance?"** build the foundation for trust.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Requesting Equipment from General Affairs

**Situation**: An needs a projector for a last-minute client demo this afternoon. He goes to see Ms. Sato in General Affairs.

**An**: Ms. Sato, thank you for your hard work. I'm An from the Sales department. Do you have a moment?

**Ms. Sato**: Yes, An-san. What's up?

**An**: Actually, we have an urgent demo at a client site this afternoon, and **I would like to borrow one projector**.

**Ms. Sato**: This afternoon? Do you have a reservation?

**An**: **I completely forgot to make a prior reservation, and I am very sorry**. I checked the schedule and it seems there is an opening; would it be possible to borrow it now?

**Ms. Sato**: Well... the rule is a reservation by the day before... Just this once, then. Please fill out this **equipment log**.

**An**: Thank you so much! **I deeply appreciate you going out of your way for me despite your busy schedule**. I will make sure to thoroughlly reserve in advance from now on to prevent this from happening again.

**Ms. Sato**: Alright, please return it by the end of business today.

**An**: Understood. **I will definitely return it by the end of the day**. Excuse me.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"I would like to borrow" (O-karishitai to kangaete orimasu)**: This uses a humble, roundabout phrasing instead of a direct command like "Give it to me."
2.  **"Forgotten" (Shitsunen shite ori)**: Admitting your mistake (forgetting the reservation) politely. Using the term **"Shitsunen"** instead of the common "Wasureta" sounds more professional and conveys a deeper sense of apology.
3.  **"Going out of your way" (Muri o kiite itadaki)**: Recognizing that your request is "unreasonable" relative to the rules, but thanking them for helping anyway. This softens the Admin staff's mood.
4.  **"Prevent this from happening again" (Tettei itashimasu)**: A promise to improve behavior, not just a shallow apology. This is how J1+ professionals build reliability.
5.  **"Definitely return it"**: Reassuring the other party. Returning equipment on time (or early) is the best way to ensure smooth requests next time.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The Logic of "Administrative Procedures" in BJT

In BJT Section 2 (Listening), interactions regarding internal rules and procedures are common.

- **Trap 1: Confusing Departments**: Where do you apply for leave? Where do you fix a PC? You must choose the department with the correct authority. Usually, **HR (Jinji)** or **IT (Joho Shisutemu)** are the answers.
- **Trap 2: How to Fill Forms**: "Put your name in the top left," "Check this box." The exam tests your ability to follow detailed administrative instructions accurately.
- **Expense Reimbursement**: BJT often features themes of **"Keihi-Seisan" (reimbursement)**. Be sure to master terms like **"Ryoushuusho" (receipt)** and **"Tatekae" (out-of-pocket payment)**.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Q1**: You want HR to issue an employment certificate for a visa application. How do you open the conversation?
A) Please make a certificate.
B) I know you're busy, but I need it right now.
C) **"I apologize for the intrusion, but I am contacting you to request the issuance of an employment certificate. What kind of procedures are necessary?"**
D) Where can I get a certificate?

**Q2**: You were warned by Admin for incorrectly sorting the trash. How do you respond?
A) Just ignore it and say "Oh, really?"
B) Refuse, saying "I'm too busy, I can't do that."
C) **"I am very sorry. It was my carelessness. From now on, I will follow the rules and sort correctly. Thank you for pointing it out."**
D) Demand to know who made such a rule.

**Q3**: You accidentally broke a company printer. What is the most professional action?
A) Stay silent and hope no one notices.
B) Complain that the printer was too old anyway.
C) **Immediately report it to the Admin staff, explain the situation, and apologize. Also, check about the repair costs.**
D) Try to take it apart and fix it yourself even though you lack the knowledge.

---

### 💡 Deep Explanation

**Q1: C**
- An apology for the interruption, a clear statement of purpose, and a confirmation of the process. This "three-set" approach marks you as a professional whom back-office staff find easy to work with.

**Q2: C**
- Trash sorting is a small thing, but it's a barometer of your teamwork and compliance. Honest admission and a promise to improve protect the company's internal harmony (Wa).

**Q3: C**
- **"Report bad news even faster."** This shows respect for company assets. Being honest about a mistake ultimately protects your own reputation.
