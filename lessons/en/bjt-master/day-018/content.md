# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 18
## 🎯 Theme: Mentoring Juniors & Feedback [Kouhai Shidou] - Secrets of Praise and Correction for Growth
### 📊 Target Level: J2 -> J1+ (Leadership & Management Foundation)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: "Praising" Must be Learned, "Correcting" Requires Art

You are mentoring a new employee (Kouhai). He makes a mistake in a step of the data entry process. You remind him: **「これ、間違っていますよ。次から気をつけてください」** [This is wrong. Be careful next time].

The Kouhai nods "Yes," but the next day he makes the exact same mistake. You get annoyed: "I already told him, so why is he still wrong?"

The problem is not the Kouhai's carelessness, but your **「指導法」 (Shidou-hou - Instruction Method)**.

In J1+ culture, the above reminder is considered **「詰めが甘い」 (Tsume ga amai - Lacking precision/follow-through)**. You only pointed out "the mistake" without pointing out "how to fix it" and "why it was wrong." Conversely, if you scold too harshly, you might violate **「パワーハラスメント」 (Power Harassment)**.

A J1+ professional knows how to turn a mistake into an **「教育の機会」 (Kyouiku no kikai - Educational Opportunity)**—a place where the Kouhai not only fixes the error but also feels grateful for your guidance.

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Bonsai Caretaker

Guiding a junior is like **Taking care of a Bonsai tree**.
- **A person at N1 level**: Is someone who only wants the tree to grow fast and straight according to their own will. If they see a branch that is crooked, they break it off immediately (direct criticism). The result is that the tree might die or grow deformed because of the trauma.
- **A person at J1+ level**: Is a true Bonsai artist. They use soft wire (subtle advice) to bend the branches slowly. They understand that every tree has its own growth rate.
    - **Bending**: This is when you give feedback.
    - **Watering**: This is when you praise.
    - **Pruning**: This is when you strictly correct an error.

A beautiful Bonsai is thanks to the patience and bending technique of the caretaker that doesn't cause breakage.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Psychology of "Harmony" and "Growth"

In Japan, guiding a Kouhai is not just about transmitting knowledge; it's about transmitting the **「組織の流儀」 (Soshiki no ryuugi - Organizational Style/Etiquette)**.

1. **The Sandwich Method**: A feedback technique.
    - **Layer 1 (Bread)**: Praise a good point (**Affirmation**).
    - **The Meat**: Point out the area that needs correction (**Criticism**).
    - **Layer 2 (Bread)**: Encourage and express expectations (**Expectation**).
2. **Praising (Homeru)**: Don't just give a vague "Great job." Praise the **Process**. Example: "I saw that you checked the document three times; that thoroughness is very valuable."
3. **Scolding (Shikaru) vs. Getting Angry (Okoru)**: Distinguish between **Correcting for work (Shikaru)** and **Losing one's temper out of emotion (Okoru)**. A J1+ professional never "Okoru." They "Shikaru" based on facts and toward the future.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Feedback to a Junior who Made a Mistake

**Situation:** Kouhai (Kim) submits a report with some formatting errors. An is the mentor.

**An:** Ms. Kim, good work. I've checked the report. **The way the data is summarized was very easy to understand; that was good**.
**Kim:** Thank you so much!
**An:** **However, there is one point I'd like you to correct**. The units in the graph remain as "Yen" instead of "1,000 Yen" in some parts. If this part is wrong, the whole set of figures will change.
**Kim:** Oh, I'm sorry! I'll fix it right away.
**An:** I think you were working while being busy, but **since your work is always thorough**, it would be a real waste if your evaluation dropped because of such small mistakes. Next time, before sending, why don't you use a checklist to re-verify just the units?
**Kim:** Yes, you are exactly right. I will be thorough next time. Thank you for pointing it out.
**An:** I'm counting on you. I'll look forward to the revised version.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Wakariyasukute yokatta desu yo"**: Starts with positive recognition. This helps the junior open their heart and be ready to listen to what follows.
2.  **"Tada itten"** (Just one point): A way to downplay the severity of the mistake, so the listener doesn't feel "attacked."
3.  **"Mottainai"** (What a waste): A very "Japanese" word. Instead of scolding "You are careless," saying "It's a waste of your effort" makes the junior feel remorseful and want to improve on their own.
4.  **"Tsukatte mimasen ka"** (Why don't you try using): Offering a solution as a **suggestion** rather than an order. This promotes the junior's initiative.
5.  **"Kitai shite imasu yo"** (I have high expectations): Ends with trust. This is a mental medicine to help the junior regain confidence after being corrected.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The "Professional Ethics" Problem on the BJT

In the BJT Section 3, there are always questions about the interaction between superior and subordinate.

- **Trap 1: Over-praising.** Exaggeratedly praising someone for correctly doing a task that is "supposed to be done" will be seen as sarcastic or unprofessional. The BJT requires **Moderation (Sesshu)**.
- **Trap 2: Giving instructions from the wrong status.** A junior "teaching" a senior (Senpai) about a wrong word usage is an absolute J1+ error. Even if the Senpai is wrong, you must use phrases like **「～ではないでしょうか」** (Isn't it the case that...?).
- **Leading Logic**: Correct answers often contain verbs like **「促す」 (Unagasu - Promote/Encourage)** or **「助言する」 (Jogen suru - Advise)**, rather than forceful ones.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: When a junior has done a task very well, how should you praise them?
- A) As expected! You're a genius.
- B) **"The structure of this material was very logical and easy to understand. I can see the traces of your hard work."**

**Question 2**: You notice a junior using the wrong honorific (Keigo) while serving a customer. What should you do?
- A) Scold them immediately in front of the customer to show your strictness.
- B) Wait until the customer leaves, then call the junior aside and gently explain the correct form.

**Question 3**: When a junior asks, "Why do I have to do this?", which answer is the most "Elite"?
- A) Because the boss said so.
- B) **"This task is an important process that ultimately becomes the foundation of Project XX. Your accurate work is necessary for this."**

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: B**. Praises the **Specific Result** and the **Effort**.
> - **Q2: B**. The golden rule: **"Correct in private."**
> - **Q3: B**. Explains the **Value of the work** and the **Importance of the individual**.

## 📌 Today's Summary

1.  **Sandwich Your Feedback**: Affirm first, then correct, then encourage.
2.  **Focus on the "Process"**: Help juniors understand the logic behind the task.
3.  **Protect Their Face**: Correction should be a growth opportunity, not a public humiliation.
