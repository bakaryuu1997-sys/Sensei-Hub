# 🧠 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Master IP Framework (Chiteki Zaisan-ken)

To be a Master, you must distinguish between types of Intellectual Property (IP):

### 3.1 Copyright (著作権)
-   **Subject**: Computer programs, databases, manuals.
-   **Feature**: Arises automatically as soon as the work is created (no registration needed).
-   **Master Rule**: In Japan, if you write code during working hours using company resources, the copyright belongs to the **Legal Entity (Company)**, not you (**Houshin Chosaku - 職務著作**).

### 3.2 Patent (特許権)
-   **Subject**: Highly creative inventions solving technical problems (e.g., a new compression algorithm).
-   **Feature**: **Must be registered** and granted by the Patent Office.
-   **Duration**: 20 years from the filing date.

### 3.3 Trademark (商標権)
-   **Subject**: Logos, product names (e.g., Oracle's "Java").

# 🏗️ 4. ARCHITECTURE & FLOW: Personal Data Protection (APPI & GDPR)

### 4.1 APPI (Act on the Protection of Personal Information - 個人情報保護法)
-   **Golden Rule**: Must notify the purpose of use and obtain consent.
-   **Sensitive Data**: Includes fingerprints, face ID, and purchase history.

### 4.2 GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)
-   **Extraterritoriality**: Even if your company is in Japan, if you serve European customers, you **must** comply with GDPR. Fines can reach 4% of global revenue.

# 👷 5. LABOR LAWS IN JAPAN: IT Contract Traps (SES vs Outsourcing)

In Japan, misinterpreting contract forms can lead to **Illegal Labor Supply (Gisou Ukeoi - 偽装請負)** violations.

### 5.1 Outsourcing (Gyoumu Itaku - 業務委託)
-   **Feature**: Company A hires Company B. Customer A **has no right** to give direct orders to you. You only follow orders from your direct boss at Company B.

### 5.2 SES (System Engineering Service)
-   **Feature**: Usually a **Quasi-mandate (Jun-in-in - 準委任)** contract. You work at the customer's office.
-   **Master Risk**: If Customer A gives you direct orders (e.g., "Please work OT today") while the contract is Gyoumu Itaku, it is a serious legal violation.

# 🔓 6. OPEN SOURCE LICENSES (OSS)

### 6.1 Permissive Licenses
-   **MIT, Apache, BSD**: You can copy, modify, and use in commercial projects. Just keep the original copyright notice.

### 6.2 Copyleft Licenses
-   **GPL (General Public License)**: If you use a GPL library in your code, you **must** open-source the entire project.
-   **LGPL**: Less restrictive; only requires opening source if you modify the library itself.
