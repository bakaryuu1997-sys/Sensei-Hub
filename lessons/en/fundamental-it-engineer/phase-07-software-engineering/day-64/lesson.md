# 🪝 1. THE HOOK: When the "Perfect Plan" Collapses

There is a famous saying in Project Management (PM): "A plan is just a list of things that will never happen."

Imagine your project is running smoothly, then suddenly the Tech Lead resigns, or the client abruptly requests a complete UI overhaul just one week before the Release date. This is the moment **Risk (リスク - Risk)** manifests.

In Japan, risk preparation is elevated to a cultural level. They don't just wait for risks to happen and then fix them; they predict them months in advance. Day 64 will teach you how to "see the future" and lead your team to safety even in the middle of a storm.

---

# 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Captain and the Transoceanic Voyage

Imagine you are the captain of the starship **Supernova**:

1.  **Project Execution**: The daily coordination of the crew—hoisting sails, cooking, and cleaning.
2.  **Risk Management (リスク管理)**: Having life jackets ready, stockpiling emergency rations, and monitoring weather reports to avoid storms.
3.  **Communication**: Using the megaphone to coordinate sailors and the radio to report back to the mainland (The Client) to keep them at ease.
4.  **Change Management (管理変更)**: When a storm hits, you must order a course correction immediately without causing panic among the crew.

Without good communication, the crew will riot. Without risk management, a single small wave can sink the ship.

# ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Risk Management Flow

In the **FE Master** exam, you must master the 4 steps of risk management:

1.  **Risk Identification (リスク特定)**: Listing everything that could go wrong.
2.  **Risk Analysis (リスク分析)**: Calculating: `Impact x Probability of Occurrence`.
3.  **Risk Response Planning (リスク対応計画)**: Developing action plans.
4.  **Risk Monitoring (リスク監視)**: Tracking risk status and triggers throughout the project.

**Master Insight**: Don't try to eliminate every single risk. Focus your energy on risks with the highest "Impact" and "Probability"!

# 🚀 4. DEEP DIVE: 4 Strategies for Negative Risks

This is a classic question in the IPA exam:

### 4.1 Avoidance (回避 - Né tránh)
Changing the project plan to eliminate the threat entirely.
*Example*: Removing a feature that is too difficult and likely to cause a deadline delay.

### 4.2 Mitigation (軽減 - Làm nhẹ)
Reducing the probability of occurrence or the impact of the risk.
*Example*: Hiring an expert to train the team, reducing the risk of a knowledge gap.

### 4.3 Transfer (転嫁 - Chuyển giao)
Shifting the risk to a third party.
*Example*: Buying project insurance or outsourcing high-risk work modules.

### 4.4 Acceptance (受容 - Chấp nhận)
Taking no action unless the risk occurs.
*Example*: Accepting that the server might lag slightly if user traffic spikes for 5 minutes.

# 🎯 5. EXAM LOGIC: IPA Japan Focus - Change & Communication

**Master Traps to Avoid:**
1.  **Stakeholders (ステークホルダ)**: Not just the client, but everyone affected by the project (Devs, Users, Managers, etc.). You must manage the expectations of all of them.
2.  **Communication Channels**: The number of communication channels is calculated as `n * (n - 1) / 2`. If a group has 10 people, there are 45 channels. The more people, the higher the "Communication Noise"!

**Sample Question (Japanese style):**
"What do you call an uncertain event that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on project objectives?"
A. Scope B. Risk C. Milestone D. Critical Path
*Answer: B.*

# 💼 6. PROFESSIONAL CONTEXT: Negotiation Skills for a Master BrSE

When a serious risk occurs, a BrSE must never say: "I'm sorry, it's our fault."
**Master SE Approach**:
1.  Report immediately (Speed is life).
2.  Present 3 Options (Option A, B, C) with pros, cons, and costs for each.
3.  Let the client choose the option that best fits their budget and priorities.
*Master Skill*: Turning an incident into an opportunity to demonstrate professionalism and problem-solving ability under high pressure.

# 🚀 4.3 Configuration Management (構成管理)

Configuration Management ensures that everything in the project (Code, Documents, Tooling) is version-controlled.
-   **Baseline (ベースライン)**: An approved version of a work product (e.g., Final Design).
-   **Change Control Board (CCB)**: A formal group responsible for approving or rejecting changes to the baselines.
-   **Avoiding "Scope Creep"**: The uncontrolled expansion of project scope without adjustments to time, cost, and resources.

### 4.4 Conflict Resolution (紛争解決) - The Art of People Management

Conflict is inevitable in project execution. A Master PM uses 5 strategies:
1.  **Collaborating (協力)**: Both parties find a Win-Win solution (The Best).
2.  **Compromising (妥協)**: Each party gives up something to reach an agreement.
3.  **Smoothing (平滑化)**: Emphasizing areas of agreement rather than differences.
4.  **Forcing (強制)**: Using power/authority to push a solution (The Worst).
5.  **Withdrawal (撤退)**: Retracting from a conflict to resolve it later.

# 📍 9. MASTER CASE STUDY: Handling "Scope Creep" in a Fintech Project

**Problem**: Mid-development, the client requested a "QR Code Payment" feature that was not in the original contract.

**Master Solution**:
1.  The PM did not refuse flatly but used the **Change Control Process**.
2.  Reported: "This feature will delay the Deadline by 2 weeks and increase costs by 5,000,000 Yen."
3.  The client agreed to cut another less important feature to keep the original launch date.

**Result**: The project launched on time with the new feature without overloading the team.

# 🎯 10. EXAM LOGIC: Calculating Risk Cost (EMV)

IPA exams often ask about **Expected Monetary Value (EMV)**.
*Master Problem*: Risk A has a 20% probability of causing a loss of 1,000,000 Yen. Risk B has a 10% probability of bringing a profit of 500,000 Yen. What is the total EMV?
**Calculation**:
-   EMV_A = 20% * (-1,000,000) = -200,000 Yen.
-   EMV_B = 10% * 500,000 = 50,000 Yen.
-   **Total EMV = -200,000 + 50,000 = -150,000 Yen**. The project should set aside 150,000 Yen as a reserve.

# 💼 12. PROFESSIONAL CONTEXT: "Kiken Yochi" Culture (Risk Prediction)

In Japanese factories and IT offices, the culture of **Kiken Yochi (危険予知 - KY)** is extremely common.
-   Before every morning, the team discusses: "Is there anything dangerous/risky today?".
-   A Master BrSE brings this to Daily Scrum: "Does anyone see any risks in Module X today?".
*Master Skill*: Building a "Safety First" culture can reduce bugs by 50% starting from the ideation phase.
