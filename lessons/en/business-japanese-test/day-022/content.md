# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 22
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Events and Socializing - Etiquette and Conduct at Drinking Parties (Nomikai)
### 📊 Target Level: J2 -> J1+ (Internal Networking Strategy)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: A "Glass of Sake" or a "Career Sentence"?

You've been invited to a **"Konshinkai" (social gathering)** to celebrate the end of a project. You arrive on time and sit in the seat designated by your boss. When your boss prepares to pour a drink for you, you hold the glass with one hand, smile, and say:
**"Thank you! Let's drink up today!"**

Throughout the evening, you talk enthusiastically about your hobbies, occasionally patting your boss on the shoulder and complimenting him:
**"Manager, you're actually quite funny!"**

The next day, your boss treats you with a smile, but you notice you are no longer being invited to important meetings.
What was your mistake? You mistook the word **"Bureiko" (drinking without rank or formality)** as a literal invitation to throw away all etiquette.

In J1+ culture, "Bureiko" is often a psychological trap. A drinking party is **"an extension of work" (Shigoto no encho)**. Every action—from how you hold your glass to how you pour and the topics you choose—is observed to evaluate your "character" (Pinkaku) and "consideration" (Hairyo).

---

## 🎭 2. THE ANALOGY: A Kabuki Stage

Participating in a Japanese company's "Nomikai" is like performing on a **Kabuki Stage**.
- **Costume**: Your attitude (even while drinking, your mindset remains that of an employee).
- **Script**: The unspoken rules (Kamiza/Shimosa seating, pouring etiquette).
- **Audience**: Your boss and senior colleagues.

On this stage, you aren't allowed to "improvise" recklessly. You must know when to pour, when to listen, and when to "open up" in a controlled manner. If you act too "real" (getting drunk, slipping up verbally), you ruin the entire performance. A J1+ master is a skilled actor who can create a fun atmosphere while maintaining the dignified boundaries of a business professional.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Psychology of "Wa" (Harmony) in a Glass

Why do the Japanese value Nomikai so highly? It’s because of the concept of **"Nominication"** (Drinking + Communication).

1. **Kamiza and Shimosa (Seating)**: The rules of seating. High-ranking individuals sit furthest from the entrance (the safest, quietest spot). Juniors sit closest to the entrance to facilitate ordering and welcoming guests.
2. **Oshaku (Pouring)**: Never let your superior's glass stay empty. However, don't fill it to the brim; 80% is the rule. Always use both hands when pouring.
3. **Henpai (Returning the Gesture)**: When a superior pours for you, take at least one sip before setting it down. Do not flatly refuse unless for extremely serious health reasons.
4. **Topic Selection**: Avoid the three "Forbidden Zones": Politics, Religion, and Criticizing those who aren't present. J1+ professionals always steer the conversation toward gratitude for their superior's guidance.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Conversation and Etiquette at a Party

**Situation**: It's the year-end party (Bonenkai). An is sitting in the "Shimosa" (junior) position and prepares to pour a drink for Manager Yamada.

**An**: Manager, how is your drink? Would you like a refill on your beer?

**Manager Yamada**: Ah, thank you. I'll take one.

**An**: **(Holds the bottle with both hands, label facing up, and pours)** Thank you for your hard work. I am truly grateful for all your support this year.

**Manager Yamada**: Not at all, An-san. You worked very hard this year. Especially that project with Company A—you were a big help.

**An**: **I am honored to hear that**. It was only because of your advice that I was able to see it through. I still have a lot to learn, but I look forward to your guidance next year as well.

**Manager Yamada**: Well, let's leave work talk aside for today and just enjoy the drinks.

**An**: Yes, thank you! By the way, I heard you enjoy golf. Have you been playing lately?

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"How is your drink?"**: A subtle opening. Don't ask "Do you want more?"; ask "How is the status of your drink?"
2.  **Bottle Etiquette**: Holding the bottle with both hands and keeping the label facing up is a small detail that shows high-level upbringing and respect.
3.  **"Grateful for your support" (O-sewa ni narimashita)**: A mandatory phrase for summarizing relationships during festive occasions.
4.  **"Leave work talk aside"**: When a boss says this, you can switch to casual topics (hobbies), but you must keep your polite speech (Keigo).
5.  **Golf Topic**: A safe and "prestigious" topic in Japanese business circles. It shows you take an interest in your superior's lifestyle.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Etiquette Problems in BJT Reading & Listening

BJT often uses social gathering situations to test your sensitivity to rank and hierarchy.

- **Trap 1: Pouring Order**: You must pour for the highest-ranking person first, then work your way down. The exam might provide a seating chart and ask who should be served first.
- **Trap 2: Refusing Alcohol**: If you can't drink, the correct answer is never "I don't drink." It should be: "Unfortunately, I am not feeling well today, so I am sticking to soft drinks. I truly appreciate the gesture, though."
- **Trap 3: The Next-Day Thanks**: A critical detail is to thank the boss again the next morning at the office: "Thank you for the meal yesterday. I had a wonderful time." BJT may ask about the next action after a party.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Q1**: When your boss is about to pour a drink for you, how should you hold your glass?
A) Leave it on the table so it's easy to pour.
B) Hold it with one hand and raise it high.
C) **Always hold the glass with both hands and tilt it slightly toward the person pouring.**
D) Tell the boss, "It's okay, I'll pour it myself."

**Q2**: You need to leave a Nomikai early. What is the most polite way to say it?
A) I'm sleepy, so I'm going home.
B) Sorry, I'll be leaving now.
C) **"I am so sorry to leave while everyone is having such a great time, but I have a commitment early tomorrow morning, so I must take my leave now."**
D) It's getting boring, so I'm leaving.

**Q3**: At a party, the boss starts complaining about another colleague. How do you respond?
A) Agree immediately to please the boss.
B) Argue with the boss to defend your colleague.
C) **Nod along to acknowledge, but avoid offering your own criticism, then subtly change the subject.**
D) Record it as evidence.

---

### 💡 Deep Explanation

**Q1: C**
- Holding with both hands is a sign of "Humility" (Kenkyo). Tilting the glass helps the drink flow smoothly without splashing—a functional consideration of J1+.

**Q2: C**
- Using the phrase "while everyone is having such a great time" compliments the atmosphere, reducing the "party-pooper" vibe of leaving early.

**Q3: C**
- In a drinking setting, a boss might slip up. If you join in, you're a "backstabber." If you argue, the boss loses face. Handling it like C protects both you and the team's harmony (Wa).
