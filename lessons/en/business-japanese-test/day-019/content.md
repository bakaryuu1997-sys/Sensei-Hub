# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 19
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Information Sharing & Confidentiality [Shanai Jouhou Kyouyuu - Hitoku Gimu] - Managing Information and Compliance
### 📊 Target Level: J2 -> J1+ (Business Ethics & Compliance Mastery)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The Fatal "Secret" in the Restroom

You are washing your hands in the restroom after work with a close colleague. You casually mention a new project your boss discussed with you this morning: **「新色のスマホ、来月発売だって。楽しみだね」** [I heard the new color smartphone is launching next month. I'm really looking forward to it].

You didn't know that in the next stall, an employee from a different department was listening. An hour later, the rumor about the "new color" spreads throughout the company, eventually reaching the ears of a competitor.

Your boss calls you in: "Why was this top-secret information leaked?"
You defend yourself: "I only told someone inside our company; I didn't tell any outsiders."

Congratulations, you have just committed a serious violation of **「秘匿義務」 (Hitoku gimu - Confidentiality Obligation)**. In a J1+ professional environment, information is not divided into "Insiders" and "Outsiders," but into **「知る必要のある人」 (Shiruhitsuyou no aru hito - Those with a need to know)** and **「それ以外」 (Everyone else)**. Disclosing information to a colleague unrelated to the project is a very heavy **「コンプライアンス」 (Compliance)** violation.

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Password-less USB Drive

Managing information in a company is like holding a **USB drive containing all of your family's assets**.
- **A person at N1 level**: Thinks that as long as they don't drop the USB on the street (don't leak it to a competitor), it's safe. They nonchalantly plug the USB into any computer in the house (sharing with any colleague).
- **A person at J1+ level**: Understands that every folder in the USB must have its own **Private Password**.
    - Salary information: Only HR's password.
    - New project info: Only the project team's password.
    - Customer info: Only the sales team's password.

Opening the "password" for the wrong person, even someone "in the house," is the same as creating a security hole. When information is "diluted," it leaks out more easily. J1+ is someone who manages the "keys" to information with the utmost discipline.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The "Need-to-Know" Principle and Legal Liability

In Japan, the concepts of **「社外秘」 (Shagai-hi - Internal Secret)** and **「守秘義務」 (Shuhi gimu - Secrecy Obligation)** are very strictly defined in employment contracts.

1. **Need-to-know**: This is the golden rule. Before sharing anything, ask yourself: "If this person doesn't know this information, will their work be affected?". If not, stay silent.
2. **Information Importance (Jouhou no juuyou-do)**: Information is often classified:
    - **Top Secret (Goku-hi)**: Known only by a few leaders.
    - **Internal Secret (Shanai-hi)**: Can be shared within the department.
    - **Public (Koukai)**: Information approved for public announcement.
3. **Insider Trading Prevention**: Disclosing information that could affect stock prices (such as M&A, new products) is not just a professional mistake but a **criminal offense**.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Confirming Information Handling

**Situation:** An receives a customer data file from the Marketing department to prepare for a new campaign. An wants to confirm if she can share this file with the Sales team.

**An:** Mr. Sato, there's something I'd like to confirm regarding the "New Product Promotion List" I received earlier.
**Sato:** Yes, what is it?
**An:** This list includes customers' personal information. **Is it alright to share this** with members of the Sales department who are not involved in this project?
**Sato:** Oh, that is **strictly forbidden**. This material has **viewing restrictions** for anyone other than the selected members of this project.
**An:** Understood. **I'm glad I checked just in case**. In that case, should I also refrain from uploading it to the internal chat (Slack)?
**Sato:** That's right. Please put it in a **password-protected folder** on the shared server. Could you give the password directly only to the person in charge?
**An:** Certainly. **I will be thorough**.
**Sato:** Thank you. Information leaks directly impact the company's credibility.
**An:** I will keep that in mind. Excuse me.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Genkin desu"** (Strictly forbidden): A very strong word. In business, when it comes to security, clarity is more important than gentleness.
2.  **"Etsuran seigen"** (Viewing restrictions): A professional term in file and system management.
3.  **"Hikaeta"** (Refrained/Restrained): An's proactive question about "refraining from uploading to chat" shows high risk-management thinking.
4.  **"Tettei itashimasu"** (I will be thorough): An absolute affirmation of compliance. In J1+, "thoroughness" is the minimum requirement for security matters.
5.  **"Kimo ni meijimasu"** (Bear in mind/Engrave in one's heart): A phrase expressing deep memorization of an instruction, usually used for extremely important matters like ethics or security.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The "Right Person - Right Job" on the BJT

In BJT Section 3 (Texts/Emails), you will encounter situations about forwarding information.

- **Trap 1: Email CC (Carbon Copy) content.** The question might ask: "Why did An CC this person?". The correct answer usually relates to the **「共有の必要性」 (Need for sharing)**. If the CC'ed person is unrelated, that answer is wrong from a compliance standpoint.
- **Trap 2: Attached documents.** If an email says "Secret document," but in the dialogue, an employee intends to print it out and leave it on their desk, that is a security violation.
- **Trap 3: Handling a leak.** If information has accidentally leaked, the correct action is always to **report to the boss and the legal team immediately**, never to stay silent and try to hide the tracks.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: You find a document labeled **「部外秘」 (Bugai-hi - Confidential to the department)** left on the photocopier. What should you do?
- A) Leave it there for the owner to find.
- B) Throw it in the trash for safety.
- C) Take it to your desk to see if there's anything interesting.
- D) **Immediately take it to the manager of that department or the administrative department for handling.**

**Question 2**: A former colleague who moved to a competitor calls you for coffee and asks: "Any new projects at your company lately?". How should you answer?
- A) Tell them everything to show off your success.
- B) **"I'm sorry, I have a confidentiality obligation, so I cannot speak about the details of my work."**

**Question 3**: When sending an email with an attached file containing personal customer data, which action is mandatory?
- A) Send the file as usual.
- B) **"Protect the file with a password and send the password in a separate email."**

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: D**. Returning it to a **responsible manager** protects the company's integrity.
> - **Q2: B**. Invoking the **confidentiality obligation** allows you to maintain friendship while not violating principles.
> - **Q3: B**. This is the standard procedure. Sending the password separately prevents a mishap if the email is sent to the wrong address.
