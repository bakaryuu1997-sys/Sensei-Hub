# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 15
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Email and Chat [Shanai Meiru - Chatto] - Balancing Efficiency and Etiquette
### 📊 Target Level: J3 -> J2+ (Digital Comm Mastery)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: "Wordiness" is "Rudeness" in the Digital Age

You send a Slack message to a colleague in the same department to ask for a document:
**「お疲れ様です。お忙しいところ申し訳ございません。もしよろしければ、この前の会議の資料を送っていただけないでしょうか。お手数をおかけしてすみませんが、よろしくお願いいたします。」**

You think you're being very polite? In reality: For close colleagues, you are being seen as **「堅苦しい」 (Katakurushii - Overly stiff/formal)** and wasting their reading time.

In J1+ culture, politeness is not measured by the number of honorifics you use, but by how much you **「相手の手間を省く」 (Aite no tema wo habaku - Save the other person's effort)**. The era of Slack and Teams requires a perfect balance between respect and response speed. Writing too much for a small task is a form of "time theft."

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Email is a Suit, Chat is a Polo Shirt

Imagine choosing your work attire.
- **Email (External/Internal Formal)**: Like a well-tailored **Suit**. You must have everything: the Tie (Subject line), Shirt (Greetings), Jacket (Main content), and Dress Shoes (Closing). It is formal and dignified but takes time to put on.
- **Chat (Slack/Teams)**: Like a **Polo Shirt**. It's still professional (Business Casual), more comfortable, and allows you to move faster. However, you still can't wear shorts or flip-flops (meaning no overly casual language or missing subjects).

Knowing when to wear a "Suit" and when to wear a "Polo Shirt" is the key situational awareness skill of a professional.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The "3-Second Rule" and the "Pyramid Structure"

Why do Japanese bosses prefer concise emails/messages? Because they have to process hundreds of messages every day.

1. **Write the Conclusion First (Ketsuron kara kaku)**: Always put the conclusion at the top. Avoid the "Because... therefore..." structure. Use the "I am reporting that X is the result. The reason is Y" structure.
2. **Bullet Points (Kajou-gaki)**: Use bullet points. Japanese professionals love seeing lists like **【1.】【2.】【3.】**. It allows them to grasp the main points in just 3 seconds of glancing.
3. **The Importance of Reactions**: In chat, using emojis like ✅ or 🙇‍♂️ is not "unprofessional"; it is the fastest way to report: **「確認しました」** [Confirmed/Received]. Silence in chat is the ultimate rudeness.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Business Communication via Chat

**Situation:** An messages Sato (IT Department) via Slack to ask about access rights.

**An:** **お疲れ様です。** **【ご相談】** (Inquiry) Regarding access rights for the new system.
**An:** Mr. Sato, excuse me for bothering you while you're busy. Regarding the request for granting permissions I made the other day, how is the current progress?
Since I want to use it for testing starting at 15:00 today, it would be a great help if it could be completed by then.
**Sato:** Ms. An, good work. Understood.
I am working on the settings now, so it is scheduled to be finished by 14:30. I'll contact you again when it's done.
**An:** **Thank you so much! That helps a lot.** 🙇‍♂️

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Otsukaresama desu"**: The "universal" greeting internally. Whether to a boss or an employee, this is the safest opening. Never use "Gokurousama desu" (which is for superiors to inferiors).
2.  **【ご相談】 (Consultation) / 【重要】 (Important)**: Using square brackets allows the receiver to know the nature of the message before even opening it. This is **Omoiyari** (Consideration).
3.  **"~Tasukarimasu"** (That helps): An encouraging way to make a request. Instead of ordering "Do it by 15:00," saying "It would help me a lot if it's done by 15:00" makes the receiver feel happier to cooperate.
4.  **Emojis (🙇‍♂️ / ✅)**: In internal Slack, using emojis is a way to shorten the psychological distance and soften a tense work atmosphere.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The "Receiver" Trap in BJT Section 3

In the reading comprehension part of the BJT, you are often tricked by the relationship between the sender and the receiver.

- **Trap 1: Using "customer-level" honorifics for your boss when talking to a client.** If an email is sent to a colleague about a meeting with a client, you must not use overly high honorifics for that colleague. The BJT tests the distinction between **「身内」 (Uchi - Insiders)** and **「外部」 (Soto - Outsiders)**.
- **Trap 2: Subject line not matching the content.** The question asks: "What is the main point?". You must find the most comprehensive answer, usually located in the first line after the greeting. Small details below are just supporting info.
- **Trap 3: Appropriate response.** A message asks: "Are you busy?". The correct answer must be a confirmation of status (Busy/Free) + a proposed time, rather than just "Yes" or "No."

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: You want to send an email to your boss to request a leave of absence. Which subject line is the best?
- A) I'm taking a day off.
- B) It's An. About my day off.
- C) **【休暇届】4月10日(水) 休暇取得のお願い（安）** [Leave Request] April 10 (Wed) Request for Leave (An)

**Question 2**: When sending a document file via chat to a superior, which phrase should accompany it?
- A) Read this.
- B) I'm sending the document.
- C) **I am attaching the materials. Could you please check them when you have a moment?**

**Question 3**: A colleague messages you for a favor, but you are in a meeting and cannot answer immediately. What should you do?
- A) Stay silent until the meeting ends (1 hour later).
- B) Set your status to "In a meeting" and if possible, send a quick: "I'm in a meeting, I'll be back after 15:00."

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: C**. Provides all info: Category, Date, and Name.
> - **Q2: C**. **"Tenpu itashimasu"** is the standard verb for attaching files.
> - **Q3: B**. In the digital world, "Disappearing" is what causes the most anxiety.
