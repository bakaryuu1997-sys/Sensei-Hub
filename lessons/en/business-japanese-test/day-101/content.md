## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Has That "Request" Crossed the Boundary?

You just realized that the deadline for an important project won't be met. You must request a schedule extension from your boss or a strict business partner.
**"I'm sorry, I couldn't make it in time. Can you wait until next week?"** (Sumimasen, chotto maniawanakute. Raishuu made matte morayemasu ka?)

If you, aiming for J1+, use language at this level, it will invite a "collapse of trust" in a business setting.
What BJT Section 1 asks of you is whether you can instantly calculate the other party's position (rank, relationship) and the weight of the request (burden), and select the optimal **"Keigo Gear."**

**Why do even N1-level learners stumble on "Requests"?**
Because simply knowing the form "te-kudasai" is insufficient.
- **"Permission" (Kyoka)**: Asking "Is it okay?" regarding what **you** will do.
- **"Request" (Irai)**: Asking the other party to "do something" for you.
The confusion between these two is the greatest source of lost points in BJT listening.

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Requests & Permissions - "Adjusting the Focus of a Camera"

Making a request in business is like **Adjusting the Focus of a Camera**.

- **Close Range (Close Colleague)**: It's easy to focus. Meaning is conveyed even with slightly informal expressions.
- **Long Distance (Important Client/Executive)**: Unless you carefully adjust the focus (Keigo), the image becomes blurred (intent is not conveyed), causing discomfort to the other party.

The expression "sasete itadakemasu ka?" is about focusing on yourself (obtaining permission), while "shite itadakemasu deshou ka?" is about focusing on the other party (prompting an action). If you turn the dial incorrectly, the business landscape is ruined in an instant.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: "Indebtedness" (Ongi) and "Psychological Distance"

### 1. Cushion Words That Don't Steal the Other Party's "No"
A J1+-level elite always prepares an "escape route" before a request.
- **"Osashitsukae nakereba"**: If it is not inconvenient.
- **"Gomuri wo shouchi de moushiagemasu ga"**: I say this knowing full well it's asking too much, but...
- **"Banshou okuriawase no ue"**: Please make every effort (when you absolutely want to ask a favor).

### 2. Sophisticated Techniques for Seeking "Permission"
Expressions to elevate the other party when asking to be allowed to do something yourself.
- **"...sasete itadakitaku zonjimasu ga, yoroshii deshou ka?"**
- **"...no bengi wo hakatte itadakereba koujin desu"** (I would be extremely happy if you could facilitate...).

### 3. The Dynamics of "Debt and Credit"
Requests in Japanese always involve the exchange of **"On" (Favor/Obligation)**.
- **"Go-on ni kisemasu"**: To make someone feel indebted (selling a favor).
- **"Isshou no fukaku"**: An expression including an apology when a request is due to one's own mistake.

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE: "Extreme Negotiation" for a Deadline Extension

### Scene: Requesting a deadline postponement from a business partner
- **You**: "Director Tanaka, I have **a matter to consult with you** (× a favor to ask) regarding the progress of this project."
- **Director Tanaka**: "Oh, is there a problem?"
- **You**: "**I say this knowing full well it's asking too much**, but due to the implementation of additional features, **could you possibly grant us a 'grace period' (yuuyo) of about three days** for the deadline?"
- **Director Tanaka**: "Three days, eh... the impact on other processes cannot be ignored, but..."
- **You**: "**If it is not inconvenient (Osashitsukae nakereba)**, I would like to **send you** the revised schedule by the end of today. I would be most grateful if you could grant us your **'generous treatment' (kandai na jochi)**."

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- **"Yuuyo itadaku"**: A very high-level Keigo for "to have someone wait for time."
- **"Kandai na jochi wo tamawaru"**: An entreaty for the person to forgive with an open heart rather than being strict.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Identifying the "Arrows of the Request" in Section 1

The most common incorrect answer pattern in BJT Section 1 is misunderstanding the **"Subject of the action."**

### ⚠️ Pattern 1: ...sasete itadakemasu ka?
- **Meaning**: Please permit **"Me (the speaker)"** to do something.
- **BJT Trap**: If this is mixed in the choices for a scene where you "ask the other party to do something," you might accidentally choose it, but this is a phrase that restricts "your own action."

### ⚠️ Pattern 2: ...shite itadakemasu ka?
- **Meaning**: I request that **"You (the listener)"** do something.
- **BJT Trap**: A slight difference in the ending—whether it is "...masu ka?" or "...itadakemasu ka?"—drastically changes the rank of politeness.

### ⚠️ Pattern 3: O (Go)-... negaemasu ka?
- **Meaning**: The most standard and formal form of a request.
- **Strategy**: Before the audio plays, predict **"who should be the one taking action in this scene."**

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Day 101 - Requests & Permissions Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
When asking your boss to attend a meeting instead of you, which is most appropriate?
- A. Watashi no kawari ni kaigi ni shusseki sasete itadakemasu ka.
- B. Watashi no kawari ni kaigi ni shusseki shite itadakenai deshou ka.
- C. Watashi no kawari ni kaigi ni shusseki shite kudasaimasu ka.
- D. Kaigi e no shusseki wo haishou itashimasu.

**Q2:**
What phrase should be added when asking a business partner to disclose information that hasn't been decided yet?
- A. Osashitsukae nakereba
- B. Go-sashuu no ue
- C. Shikarubeki jochi to shite
- D. Haishou itashitaku

**Q3:**
You said, "Could you please permit me to proceed with this matter at my own discretion?" Who is "proceeding"?
- A. The other party (boss)
- B. Yourself (speaker)
- C. A third party
- D. Everyone

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **B**: Since you are asking the boss to perform the **"action (attending)"**, "...shite itadakenai deshou ka" is correct. A means seeking permission to be absent yourself.
2. **A**: A "cushion" like "Osashitsukae nakereba" is essential for requests that burden the other party.
3. **B**: The form **"O (Go)-... itadaku"** means receiving a benefit from the other party, and the subject is yourself.
