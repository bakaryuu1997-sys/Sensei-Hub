## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: "Elite Vocabulary" for Crisis Management and Negotiation

### 1. Expressing the Severity of the Situation
- **"Jindai na"**: Enormous/Immense (damage, impact, etc.). "The damage is immense."
- **"Doronuma-ka suru"**: To become a quagmire (to continue to deteriorate with no prospect of resolution).
- **"Kikkin no kadai"**: An urgent/pressing issue that must be dealt with immediately; the most important task.

### 2. Toward Resolution and Resurgence (Saiki)
- **"Shuuseku ni mukau"**: For chaos to start settling down; to move toward a resolution.
- **"Shakumey"**: To explain one's position or reasons and seek understanding (Vindication/Explanation).
- **"Kendo-chourai"**: For someone who has failed once to regain their momentum and attack again (A comeback).

### 3. Haggling and Tactics in Negotiation
- **"Ingin-burei"**: To be polite in words but very rude in attitude (Sarcastic negotiation).
- **"Dakyouten"**: A compromise point where both parties can be satisfied.

## 🎭 SIMULATED SCENARIO: "Total Management" of a PR Crisis

### Step 1: Grasping the Situation via Audio (Section 1)
- **PR Officer**: "Posts about the defect are spreading on the web, and voices calling for a **vindication (Shakumei)** conference are strengthening. If left alone, the brand damage will be **immense (Jindai)**!"

### Step 2: Impact Analysis via Charts (Section 2)
- **Data**: The number of dissatisfied tweets in the past 24 hours has increased "logarithmically." The stock price has fallen below the **break-even point**, and there is no **prospect (medo)** of emerging from the downward trend.

### Step 3: Legal Judgment via Reading (Section 3)
- **Lawyer's Memo**: "Legally, the **probability (Gaizensei)** of liability for damages is low, but **in view of (kan-gami-re-ba)** the loss of social credibility, I cannot help but **suggest (shisa)** a prompt voluntary recall."

**🧠 Your Integrated Judgment:**
"Prioritize the recovery of social credibility over legal correctness (**Hyouri-ittai**) and decide on an official apology and recall immediately. This is the **urgent issue (Kikkin no kadai)**."

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: "Mental Stamina" to Win Through the Final Case Study

### ⚠️ Strategy 1: "Weighting" Information
Not all information is equally important. If the question asks, "What is the most appropriate response?" make **"Section 3 (Legal/Logical Grounds)"** your final anchor. Do not be swayed by audio (emotions).

### ⚠️ Strategy 2: "Cross-Referencing" Keywords
Compare the word "carefully" from the audio with the fact of a "sharp drop" in the charts. The **"contradiction (gap)"** that arises there is the hint to the correct answer that the examiner is aiming for.

### ⚠️ Strategy 3: The "Deep Breath" Ritual
Before entering a large problem set, close your eyes for just 3 seconds and take a deep breath. It's a switch to turn your brain from "One-question-one-answer mode" to "Management meeting mode."

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Day 108 - Case Study 2 Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
You received a report: "The damage is **'Jindai'**, but the situation is moving toward **'Shuuseki'**." What is the current situation?
- A. Damage is small, and it is still chaotic.
- B. Damage is large, but it is starting to settle down.
- C. Nothing has happened.
- D. It is predicted that great damage will occur from now on.

**Q2:**
When a negotiation partner is taking an **"Ingin-burei"** attitude, what should you be careful about?
- A. Since the partner is very kind, believe everything.
- B. Be wary of aggressive negotiation intent or malice behind the polite words.
- C. Since the partner is angry, keep apologizing.
- D. Since the partner is tired, recommend a break.

**Q3:**
What is the meaning of the determination to launch a new project with the hope of **"Kendo-chourai"**?
- A. Cancel the project.
- B. Use past failures as a spring to aim for success again.
- C. Leave it to someone else.
- D. Move to the countryside and start farming.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **B**: A combination of **"Jindai" (large)** and **"Shuuseki" (settling down)**.
2. **B**: **"Ingin-burei"** refers to an aggressive attitude that uses Keigo as a weapon.
3. **B**: **"Kendo-chourai"** is a four-character idiom (Yojijukugo) expressing a strong determination for a comeback.
