# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 2
## 🎯 Theme: Bowing [Ojigi] and Business Card Exchange [Meishi Koukan] - The Physics of Trust

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The "Rating" Decided in 0.5 Seconds

Imagine walking into a meeting room with your boss to meet a partner. You reach out to shake hands in an international style, but the other person is already bending down in a bow. You awkwardly withdraw your hand and hurriedly bow back. During the exchange of business cards, you hold your card with one hand and hand it over while they are cradling theirs with both hands.

Within the first 3 minutes, even though you haven't spoken a word of Japanese, the partner has silently labeled you as **"Kyouiku-busoku"** (lacking advanced professional training).

**Why do even N1 holders feel "awkward"?**
Honorifics can be learned from books, but **"Shosa"** (physical gestures and mannerisms) cannot. The BJT exam tests your ability to control your body to match your words. A perfectly grammatical sentence like **"Moushiwake gozaimasen"** (I am deeply sorry) becomes meaningless if your bow is only 15 degrees deep.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Business Cards - A Backup of the Soul

In Japanese business culture, a business card is not just a piece of paper with contact info. Think of it as a **Hardware Token (Authentication Module)** containing the prestige of an entire corporation.

- Damaging or wrinkling a partner's business card is like corrupting their data memory.
- Placing a card on the table carelessly is like handling sensitive data without encryption.

When you hold a business card with both hands, you are not holding a piece of paper; you are cradling the **Face (Kao)** of the other person. The BJT tests whether you understand the "physical weight" of things that are inherently light, such as a piece of paper or a bow.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Mechanics of Angles and Timing

### 1. The Three Stages of Bowing (Ojigi)
The BJT requires you to clearly distinguish three types of bows based on the angle:
1. **Eshaku (15°)**: Used when passing someone in the hallway. This is a "Lightweight" greeting.
2. **Keirei (30°)**: The gold standard for welcoming guests or entering a meeting room. This is the angle of professional respect.
3. **Saikeirei (45°)**: Reserved for deep gratitude or a profound apology (**Shazai**). This is a "Heavyweight" bow.

### 2. The Aesthetics of "Simultaneous Exchange" (Douji Koukan)
In BJT scenarios, traps often lie in who hands it over first and who hands it lower.
- **Superior-Subordinate Rule**: A person of lower status (**Me-shita**) must present their card at a **lower position** than the card of the person of higher status (**Me-ue**).
- **Physical Coordination**: If both parties present at the same time, support your card case with your left hand, extend the card with your right, and then immediately use both hands to receive the partner's card. This is J1+ level physical coordination.

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: The Ritual of Card Exchange

### Scene 1: Correct Card Exchange
- **Yamada (Subordinate):** Hajimemashite. ABC Co., Ltd. de eigyou wo tantou shite orimasu Yamada to mōshimasu. Yoroshiku onegaishimasu. (Hands card at a lower position)
- **Sato (Superior):** XYZ Co., Ltd. no Sato desu. Kochira koso, yoroshiku onegaishimasu.

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- Yamada uses the phrase **"eigyou wo tantou shite orimasu"** to clearly define his role, making it easier for the partner to open the next line of conversation.
- Yamada handing the card lower is an implicit signal acknowledging Sato's status, creating a sense of psychological comfort for the partner.

### Scene 2: Behavior After Receiving the Card
- **Sato:** (Receiving the card) Yamada-sama de irasshaimasu ne. Choudai itashimasu.
- **Yamada:** Hai. Sato-buchou, honjitsu wa o-jikan wo itadaki, makoto ni arigatou gozaimasu.

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- Sato repeating Yamada's name with the honorific **"-sama"** is a confirmation signal that the data from the partner has been correctly received.
- Yamada immediately transitions to thanking him for his time (**o-jikan wo itadaki**) to shift from the ritual to the main agenda.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Decoding J1+ Level Traps

### ⚠️ Trap 1: Placement of Business Cards (Meishi no okibasho)
In Reading or visual scenario questions:
- **Situation**: You have received the card and sat down at the meeting table.
- **Common Mistake**: Putting it away in your card case immediately to avoid losing it.
- **Elite Logic**: You must place the card on top of your card case, facing the person across from you. If there are multiple people, arrange them in the order they are seated so you don't forget their names. Putting it away immediately is seen as an act of wanting to cut off the connection.

### ⚠️ Trap 2: Card Exchange at a Standing Party (Konshinkai)
The BJT loves social settings like standing parties.
- **Trap**: Your right hand is holding a wine glass. What should you do when a partner approaches?
- **Elite Logic**: Never hand over a card with a hand that is wet from holding a glass. You must put the glass down, wipe your hand dry with a napkin, and then perform the ritual. The BJT tests your preparation (**Nemawashi**) in every small detail.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Elite Practice Challenge

**Question 1:**
When performing a **Keirei (30-degree bow)**, where should your eyes (**Shisen**) be directed?
- A. Look directly into the partner's eyes to show confidence.
- B. Look down at your own toes.
- C. Look at a spot about 1 meter in front of your toes.
- D. Close your eyes to focus on the greeting.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: C**. Looking about 1m ahead helps your neck and back form a straight, professional line. Looking directly (A) is rude, and looking at your own feet (B) is a sign of timidity.

**Question 2:**
You are about to exchange cards with a partner but notice your card has a small smudge on one corner. What should you do in this BJT scenario?
- A. Hand it over anyway and pretend not to notice.
- B. Apologize saying "Yogorete orimashite shitsurei itashimasu" and give it anyway.
- C. Do not hand it over; apologize saying you ran out of cards: "Ainiku meishi wo kirashite orimashite" and follow up later via email.
- D. Quickly wipe it with your sleeve and hand it over.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: C**. In Elite culture, giving a dirty card is an insult. The "polite lie" (Ainiku...) is a safer way to save face for both parties.

**Question 3:**
When receiving a customer's card with a name written in extremely difficult Kanji, which handling shows J1+ level?
- A. Guess the reading using On-yomi.
- B. Don't read it out loud; just thank them and put it away.
- C. "Shitsurei desu ga, o-namae wa nan to oyomi sureba yoroshii deshou ka?" and take note immediately.
- D. Wait for your boss to call their name so you know how to say it.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Correct Answer: C**. Asking directly but politely is more highly valued than mispronouncing a name. The BJT values **Accuracy** over shallow pride.
