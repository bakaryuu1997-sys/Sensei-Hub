# 🌿 Day 094: Creating Shared Value (CSV) & The New Corporate Purpose

The very reason for a business's existence is being questioned at its core. The transition from the old "Shareholder Primacy" to **"Stakeholder Capitalism,"** which considers all interested parties, is an inevitable trend in modern management. Professionals at the BJT J1+ level are required to possess the ability to explain **"CSV (Creating Shared Value)"**—solving social issues and generating profit through business, rather than as mere charitable CSR—with high logical consistency. Today, we deepen our discussion on **"Purpose"** (the reason for existence), which can be called the "soul" of a company.

---

## 🪝 THE HOOK: A "Decisive Battle" at the Shareholders' Meeting

You are the Executive Officer in charge of strategy for a major consumer goods manufacturer. At the general meeting of shareholders, a sharp question flies from an institutional investor:
"Your company is investing heavily in the development of new eco-friendly materials, but won't that damage short-term profits? Is there not a risk that this will end as mere 'good-looking social contribution'?"

To this question, an emotional response like "Because it's for society" will not pass at the J1+ level.
- How are you **"Synthesizing/Sublating" (Aufheben)** profit and social contribution?
- How does this lead directly to the **"Improvement of Corporate Value"** in the long term?
- And how is it rooted in the company's **"Purpose"**?

You must speak with "dispassionate logic" and "passionate aspiration" to convince investors.

---

## 🎭 ANALOGY: Comparing a Company to a "Tree"

Let's compare corporate sustainability to a single giant **"Tree"** (Jumoku).

- **"Profit"** is the **"Fruit"** that ripens that year. Without fruit, the tree cannot be maintained.
- **"Purpose"** is the **"Roots"** buried deep in the ground. If the roots rot, even the most magnificent branches and leaves will wither.
- **"CSV"** is the **"Photosynthesis Cycle"** itself, where the tree grows while releasing oxygen and enriching the soil.

Instead of separating "enriching society (improving soil)" and "growing oneself (harvesting fruit)," think of them as a single ecosystem. This is the essence of Purpose-driven management.

---

## ⚙️ UNDER THE HOOK: The Lexical Background of Purpose Management

Japan has long had the philosophy of **"Sanpo-yoshi"** (Good for the seller, good for the buyer, good for society), but in modern global standards, more strategic expressions are preferred.

1. **"Purpose" (Reason for existence)**: Functions not as a mere slogan, but as the "North Star" for decision-making.
2. **"Dual Materiality"**: The concept of emphasizing both the impact a company has on society and the impact social changes have on the company.
3. **"Vigilance against 'Wash' (Pretense)"**: "Greenwashing" or "Purpose-washing"—where words are not accompanied by substance—causes fatal damage to the brand. In J1+, expressions questioning this "Effectiveness" (Jikkousei) become important.

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE (Board of Directors Meeting)

**Characters:**
- **Chairman Sato**: A cautious chairman from the founding family.
- **CEO Suzuki**: A transformative leader promoting Purpose-driven management.
- **Director Tanaka**: An external director with a sharp perspective.

**(At a meeting to formulate the next Medium-Term Management Plan)**

**Chairman Sato**: "President Suzuki, you've placed 'Creating social value through plastic-free initiatives' at the core of the new plan, but I'm concerned it might lead to a **'Divergence'** (Kairi) from shareholder expectations due to cost increases."

**CEO Suzuki**: "Chairman, your concern is valid. However, ignoring the international megatrend of plastic regulation will, in the long term, lead to a **'Dilution'** (Kikaku-ka) of our company's market share. What we should undertake is not a measure for simple cost-up, but a shift to **'CSV'** where new material development becomes the source of competitive advantage."

**Director Tanaka**: "Exactly. The market is demanding a clarification of the **'Purpose'**—what kind of company you are and what you value. Articulating the process of **'Sublation/Synthesis'** (Shiyou)—how to balance profit and social problem-solving at a high level—is **'Vital'** (Kanyo) in dialogue with investors."

**CEO Suzuki**: "Indeed. We will redefine our reason for existence from 'providing convenience' to 'building a circular society.' This is an **'Urgent/Pressing Issue'** (Kikkin no kadai) involving the very foundation of management."

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1. **"Sublate/Synthesize" (Shiyou)**: Integrating two contradictory things at a higher level. A word used for the highest level of intellectual dialogue in J1+.
2. **"Divergence/Gap" (Kairi)**: The separation of a connection. Used to express the gap between expectations and reality, or plans and results.
3. **"Dilution" (Kikaku-ka)**: Thinning out. Refers to the situation where brand power or market share is gradually lost.
4. **"Vital/Essential" (Kanyo)**: Being extremely important. It carries a stronger nuance than just "important," implying "intrinsically indispensable."
5. **"Urgent/Pressing Issue" (Kikkin no kadai)**: A problem that is imminent and must be hurried. A term referring to the top priority in a management meeting.

## 🧠 EXAM LOGIC: Deciphering the "Logical Construction" of BJT J1+

In questions related to "Corporate Social Responsibility," the following contrast structures often appear:

- **"CSR (Philanthropy)" vs "CSV (Solving through business)"**: BJT tends to favor the latter, "the simultaneous realization of economic and social value," as the correct answer.
- **"Mid-to-Long-term Perspective"**: Instead of denying immediate profit, you must follow the logical progression of "what choice should be made now for future sustainability."
- **"Diversity of Stakeholders"**: A perspective that is omnidirectional—including not only shareholders but also employees, local communities, customers, and the global environment (**"Silent Stakeholders"**)—is appropriate for a J1+ answer.

## 📝 QUICK QUIZ

**Q1: What do you call it when a company solves social issues through its business and simultaneously creates economic value?**
A. CSR
B. CSV
C. BCP
D. DX

**Q2: At a meeting, the opinion was raised that "Brand power is being diluted." What is the most appropriate action to improve this situation?**
A. Temporarily increase advertising expenses.
B. Redefine the company's Purpose and issue a consistent message.
C. Significantly lower the price of products.
D. Criticize rival companies.

**Q3: The CEO said, "The resolution of this issue is vital (Kanyo)." What is the CEO's correct intention?**
A. This issue can be ignored.
B. This issue is only slightly important.
C. This issue will take time to resolve.
D. This issue is an extremely important matter involving the essence of management.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **B. CSV**: A keyword in management strategy at the J1+ level.
2. **B**: The standard response to dilution (thinning of the brand) is re-confirming the foundation (Purpose) and ensuring consistency.
3. **D**: **"Kanyo"** refers to the "liver" (Kimo) and the "pivot" (Kaname)—the most critical parts.

- **"Materiality" (Key Issues)**: Issues the company should prioritize.
- **"Carbon Neutral"**.
- **"Corporate Governance"**.

By mastering the "high-dimensional language" required to remain a company needed by society, your leadership will win deeper trust.
