# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 13
## 🎯 Theme: Internal Coordination [Shanai Chousei] - Advanced Techniques for Inter-departmental Negotiation and Nemawashi
### 📊 Target Level: J2 -> J1 (Professional Strategy)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: When "Common Work" Becomes a "Personal Barrier"

You receive instructions from your boss: "Go ask the IT department to grant new system access rights to our team within this week."
You head to the IT office confidently and say: **「来週までに権限をください。部長の指示です」** [Please grant access by next week. This is the Manager's instruction].

The Result: The IT Manager frowns, and the IT staff mumbles: "Everyone wants it 'immediately'; we aren't machines, you know." Your access rights are put on hold indefinitely with the reason "currently in the queue for processing."

What was your mistake? You brought **「正論」 (Seiron - A correct but blunt argument)** to force others, forgetting the process of **「社内調整」 (Shanai Chousei - Internal Coordination)**. In a Japanese company, being "Right" is not enough; you must make the other party **「納得」 (Nattoku - Convinced/Consented)** and feel respected. J1+ pros don't use power to push; they use subtlety to "open the way."

---

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: The Gears of a Giant Machine

A company is like a system of **Gears**.
- Each department is a gear with a different size and rotation speed.
- **Shanai Chousei** is the **Lubricating Oil**.

If you intentionally shove an iron bar (a sudden request) between the rotating gears, the system will break or jam. A good internal coordinator is someone who knows where to apply oil and how to "loosen" tense positions so that the gears fit together and move without noise or damage.
The goal of Chousei is not win-lose, but **「全体最適」 (Zentai Saiteki - Overall Optimization)**.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The Art of "Debit-Credit" in Internal Relations

In Japan, the concept of **「貸し借り」 (Kashi-kari - Lending and Borrowing)** is used not only for money but also for **FAVORS**.

1. **Shanai Chousei**: Is the art of balancing interests. When you ask another department for help, you are "Borrowing" (Kari). An effective worker is someone who always accumulates "Lending" (Kashi) by supporting others before they need them.
2. **Otoshidokoro**: The "landing point"—the compromise point that both sides can accept. Never demand 100%; prepare a 70-80% plan so the other party has a way out.
3. **Kao wo tateru (Saving Face)**: Making the other party feel they are the most important person who can solve this. Example: **「IT部の佐藤さんにしか頼めないんです」** [Only Mr. Sato from IT can handle this].

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Requesting Another Department

**Situation:** An (Sales) needs to ask Sato (Production) to accelerate an order for a VIP customer.

**An:** Mr. Sato, I'm sorry to bother you when you're busy, but may I have a moment of your time?
**Sato:** Oh, Ms. An. What is it?
**An:** Actually, we received an additional order from Company A, and they've inquired if we can move the delivery date forward by one week.
**Sato:** One week? Hmm, that's quite tough with our current operations. Other projects are piled up too.
**An:** **I am well aware that this is a sudden request**. However, if this case succeeds, there's a high possibility it will lead to a large-scale contract next term.
**Sato:** Even if you say so...
**An:** Therefore, **as a special case this time**, could you consider options like outsourcing part of the process? Of course, our Sales department will take full responsibility for handling the cost adjustment for that.
**Sato:** If you go that far, Ms. An... I'll try consulting with the factory.
**An:** Thank you so much! **I know I'm asking for the impossible**, but thank you in advance for your help.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Juuju shōchi shite orimasu"** (I am well aware): A high-level variation of "I know well." Using this shows you deeply understand the other's difficulty, reducing their defensiveness.
2.  **"Konkai wa toku-rei toshite"** (As a special case this time): Creates a sense of "only this once." This helps the other party agree more easily because they don't fear setting a bad precedent.
3.  **"Sekinin wo motte taiou sasete itadakemasu"**: This is the key to coordination. You don't just bring trouble; you bring a solution and a commitment to responsibility.
4.  **"Go-muri wo moushiagemasu ga"** (I know I'm being unreasonable): A classic phrase when you know your request is excessive but you must ask. It's a powerful "lubricant" in communication.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The "Local Interest" Trap on the BJT

In BJT Section 3 (Integrated Situations), you will encounter emails or dialogues about internal conflict.

- **The Trap: Choosing the plan most beneficial to yourself.** The BJT will offer a plan that helps you finish your boss's task fastest but creates enemies in other departments. This answer is always WRONG.
- **The Correct Answer**: Always the plan showing **「配慮」 (Hairyo - Consideration)** and **「調整」 (Coordination)**. For example: Apologizing in advance, or proposing an alternative solution beneficial to both.
- **Keywords**: Look for words like **「すり合わせ」 (Suri-awase - Fine-tuning/Coordination)**, **「妥協点」 (Dakyou-ten - Compromise point)**.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Question 1**: When going to another department to ask for urgent support, what is the first thing you should do?
- A) Say immediately that your boss is in a big hurry.
- B) Apologize for the suddenness and ask if they are busy.
- C) Put the documents on their desk and say "Help me with this."

**Question 2**: Which statement best demonstrates the technique of "Kao wo tateru" (Saving face for the other party)?
- A) This is easy work; you'll finish it in no time.
- B) I asked many places but everyone refused, so I'm asking you.
- C) Regarding this matter, we cannot proceed without hearing the opinion of an expert like Mr. Sato.
- D) It's an instruction from above, so please do it.

**Question 3**: If another department refuses your request because they are too busy, what should you do?
- A) Get angry and report their lack of cooperation to your boss.
- B) Show understanding of their situation, then propose a different timeframe or reduce the scope of the request.

> **Explanation:**
> - **Q1: B**. Respect for others' time is the first step.
> - **Q2: C**. Elevates the other party to "expert" status.
> - **Q3: C**. This is the spirit of **Shanai Chousei**. You don't push; you adjust.
