# 📚 BJT Master Business Japanese (N1 Elite Edition) - Day 26
## 🎯 Theme: Proxy Response - Professional Handling and Handover for Absent Colleagues
### 📊 Target Level: J3 -> J2+ (Team Coordination & Support Mastery)

---

## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: The "Irresponsible" Response of Just Passing a Message

You were watching the office phone while your colleague Sato (who is out) received a call from Client A. Client A is in a great hurry, saying, "I need to confirm the order quantity right now!"

You answered:
**"Sato is away from his desk. I'll let him know when he's back."**

Two hours later, Sato returns, but you are busy with your own work and forget to tell him immediately. Then Client A calls again, shouting: "I've been waiting for two hours! Why hasn't anyone contacted me?"
Sato is confused: "I'm very sorry. I just found out that you called..."

Congratulations. You have simultaneously destroyed Sato's reliability and the company's credibility.
Your mistake wasn't just "forgetting to tell him." It was that you didn't understand the essence of **"Proxy Response" (Dairi Taiou)**. You lacked that one simple question: "Is it urgent?" or "Is there anything I can help you with?"

In the world of J1+, when a colleague is away, you are the company's only **"Window" (Madoguchi)**. You must not be a mere passive "messenger." You should be a professional "proxy" who reassures the client in your colleague's stead.

---

## 🎭 2. THE ANALOGY: The Backup Goalkeeper

Imagine the company as a soccer team. Your teammate (a forward) has temporarily left the pitch (for an outing or vacation).

- **The N1-Level Employee**: Just stands by and watches the ball go into the net (letting the client suffer) because "it's not my position." They just say, "I'm not the forward, so I can't score."
- **The J1+ Level Master**: Is the **"Backup Goalkeeper."**
    - Even if it's outside their specialty, they dive out to protect the goal (temporarily capturing information).
    - They never allow a "goal" (client dissatisfaction).
    - Then, when the main keeper returns, they pass the ball (the case) in the safest way possible (detailed notes).

A strong team is not a team with one star, but a team where everyone can cover for each other when someone is missing.

---

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: The J1+ Art of Covering

When handling for an absent colleague, there are three "golden rules" to protect the collective reliability of the organization:

1. **Clear Explanation of the Situation**: Don't just say they are "away." State specifically when they are expected back. **"He is scheduled to return to the office around 3:00 PM."**
2. **Proactive Proposal to Call Back**: Don't wait for the client to ask, "When will he call me?" Be proactive: **"Shall I have him call you back as soon as he returns?"**
3. **The 5W1H Message Memo**: Client name, phone number, time of call, urgent content, priority level. A J1+ professional sticks a note on the colleague's monitor or shares it immediately via chat so they see it the moment they return.
4. **Prohibition of Ultra Vires Acts (Exceeding Authority)**: Proxy response doesn't mean you can decide everything. Avoid saying "Yes" or "No" to things involving contracts if you aren't familiar with them. Use: **"After confirming, the person in charge will contact you."**

## 🎭 4. SIMULATED DIALOGUE: Handling a Phone Call for a Colleague

**Situation**: An takes a phone call for his colleague Sato. Client Tanaka is calling.

**An**: Thank you for calling. This is An from Supernova Co., Ltd.

**Tanaka**: Thank you for your continued support. This is Iwai from Tanaka Shoji. Is Sato-san there?

**An**: Mr. Iwai, thank you for your continued support. Unfortunately, Sato is **currently out** and is scheduled to return to the office around 4:00 PM.

**Tanaka**: I see. Actually, I have something I need to confirm urgently regarding the quote.

**An**: I see. **If it's something I can understand, I would be happy to assist you. How does that sound?**

**Tanaka**: I just want to know if the amount on page 3 of the document includes tax or not.

**An**: **I have a copy of that document here, so I will check it right now**. One moment please... Yes, thank you for waiting. These amounts are all tax-inclusive.

**Tanaka**: Oh, that's a relief. Thank you very much.

**An**: Not at all. **Just in case, I will let Sato know that I received a call from you when he returns.**

**Tanaka**: Yes, please do. Goodbye.

---

### 🔍 Nuance Analysis

1.  **"Unfortunately... he is out" (Ainiku... gashutsu shite orimashite)**: Using "Ainiku" (Unfortunately) expresses empathy with the client for not being able to reach the person they wanted.
2.  **"If it's something I can understand, I would be happy to assist"**: A very subtle offer of support. You don't promise to solve everything; you promise to "receive and handle" what you can.
3.  **"I have a copy here" (Temoto ni hikae ga gozaimasu)**: This shows that your team works closely together and that documents are shared transparently within the team.
4.  **"Just in case, I will let him know"**: Even if the problem is solved, informing the colleague is a perfect "covering" action, preventing the colleague from being surprised if the client calls again later.
5.  **"Let him know" (Moushi-tsutaeru)**: Correct use of humble language to the client while keeping the internal member (Sato) in a subordinate position relative to the client.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: The Logic of "Substitution" in BJT Listening Section 2

In BJT Section 2 (Listening), situations involving a third party are very common.

- **Trap 1: Confusion over Schedules**: You hear "Sato is away until the afternoon," but later it's said "Sato will go straight home without returning to the office" (**Chokki**). Question: "When can the client meet Sato?" The correct answer is "Tomorrow morning."
- **Trap 2: Who is performing the action?** "I'll have Sato call back" vs. "I'll call Sato." These are completely different actions. BJT tests whether you can identify who does what using particles and keigo.
- **Trap 3: Handling Difficult Questions**: How does a staff member react to a client's difficult question? The correct answer is always **"I will confirm" (Kakunin itashimasu)**, never "I don't know" or a random guess.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ

**Q1**: A visitor comes to see your boss, but the boss is in a meeting. What should you say?
A) The Manager is busy with a meeting right now.
B) Sorry, it's impossible right now.
C) **"I am very sorry. Unfortunately, the Manager is currently in a meeting. It is scheduled to end in about an hour. How would you like to proceed?"**
D) Sit and wait until it's finished.

**Q2**: Which memo for a colleague is the most professional?
A) "Mr. A called you."
B) **"10:15 Mr. Tanaka from Company A. Urgent callback requested regarding the quote (by 3 PM). Tel: 012-345. (Received by: An)"**
C) "You have a phone call."
D) Remember it and tell them verbally later.

**Q3**: A client insists on talking to your colleague (who went home sick), even after you offered help. What should you do?
A) Give the colleague's personal cell phone number to the client.
B) Tell the client, "He went home, call back tomorrow."
C) **"He has left early today due to poor health. If it is an urgent matter, shall I contact his mobile and have him call you back?"** (Note: Only if truly urgent and you have the colleague's permission).
D) Grumble because the client is being too difficult.

---

### 💡 Deep Explanation

**Q1: C**
- Provides full information: Reason for absence (meeting), Return time (in about an hour), and leaves the decision to the visitor. J1+ always keeps the client in control.

**Q2: B**
- A standard J1+ memo must have **5W1H**. It saves your colleague preparation time and lets them know the gravity of the situation immediately upon picking up the paper.

**Q3: C**
- This is the flexible handling of a J1+. However, note: **Never give out a colleague's personal number to a client (Security/Privacy)**. You acting as a "hub" protects the colleague's privacy while solving the client's problem.
