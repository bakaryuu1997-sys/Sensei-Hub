## 🪝 1. THE HOOK: Numbers Don't Lie, but Listening Might

You are currently looking at a line graph (**Oresen Graph**) showing sales trends. From January to June, the graph line is rising beautifully to the upper right. However, the audio from the speaker announces:
**"In our initial forecast, we expected a steady upward trend (Migikata-agari), but in reality, a state of 'marking time' (Ashibumi-joutai) has continued..."**

BJT asks you: which graph among the options most correctly represents the "actual state mentioned"?
- A. A graph where sales are growing steadily.
- B. A graph where sales surged and then plummeted.
- C. A graph where sales are stagnant (flat), neither increasing nor decreasing.
- D. A graph where sales surged at the very end.

**Why do even N1-level learners "fall"?**
N1-level learners tend to react to words like **"Expectation" (Kitai)** or **"Upward Trend" (Migikata-agari)**, unconsciously choosing the shape they "want to see" (Confirmation Bias). However, the true keyword is **"Ashibumi-joutai"** (a state of treading water/stagnation). Section 2 is extremely cunning because it intentionally creates a **"presumed contradiction"** between the visual information (the graph you see) and the auditory information (the words you hear) to shake the examinee.

If you cannot perfectly synchronize your eyes (analyzing the graph) and ears (hearing the fluctuations) at the same time, you will not reach the correct answer.

## 🎭 2. ANALOGY: Section 2 - The Orchestra Conductor

Solving BJT Section 2 is similar to playing the role of an **Orchestra Conductor**.

- **Vision (Graph)**: The "Full Score" (Score) on the desk. This is a stationary framework—static data.
- **Hearing (Audio)**: The "Music" actually ringing out from the instruments. This is dynamic data, which includes arrangements, improvisations, and sometimes "errors" different from the score.

An excellent conductor doesn't just look at the score (the diagram) but listens to the sounds actually being played (the words) and instantly grasps the "gaps" or "points of emphasis." In BJT, the graph is a "waiting trap," and the words are the "guides to the truth." The ability to control two information streams simultaneously and make a split-second judgment is required.

## ⚙️ 3. UNDER THE HOOD: Language for Controlling "Change" and "Comparison" in Data

### 1. The Gradation of Vocabulary Expressing "Transitions" (Sui-i)
An elite listener doesn't just hear "increased" or "decreased" but distinguishes the "degree":
- **"Slight Increase" (Bizou)**: A very small increase.
- **"Surge/Sharp Increase" (Kyuutou/Kyuuzou)**: A violent increase.
- **"Flat/Stagnant" (Yokobai)**: A state with no change.
- **"Hitting Bottom" (Soko wo utsu)**: A sign that a decline has stopped and is turning toward an increase.
A single-character prefix like **"Bi"** (Slight) or **"Kyuu"** (Sharp/Sudden) becomes the decisive factor in narrowing down the correct option.

### 2. Correlation in "Comparisons" (Hikaku)
In problems comparing data between Company A and Company B, the following expressions frequently appear:
- **"To exceed/surpass" (Uwamawaru)**.
- **"To fall below/be lower than" (Shitamawaru)**.
- **"To fall short of" (Oyobanai)**.
The trap is the "reversal of subject and predicate." Cool-headedness is essential to hear "A surpasses B" while not being misled by a graph showing "B is higher."

### 3. Structural Analysis of "Breakdowns" (Uchiwake)
In pie charts (**En Graph**), the most important words are **"Majority" (Kahan-suu)** (exceeding 50%) and **"To occupy/account for" (Shimeru)**.
If you are told, **"Company A accounts for one-third of the total,"** you need the mathematical instantaneousness to imagine a 120-degree sector.

## 🎭 SIMULATED DIALOGUE: A Discussion While Looking at a Graph

### Scene: Sales Analysis of a New Product
- **Manager**: "It seems the 'start-off' (Suberidashi) for this new product was strong?"
- **Staff**: "Yes. Immediately after launch, it grew explosively (**Bakuhatsu-teki ni**), but from the second month, competitors began releasing similar products (**Ruiji-hin**). Currently, as shown in the graph, growth is 'stagnating' (**Nobinayande orimasu**)."

**💡 Nuance Analysis:**
- **"Start-off" (Suberidashi)**: The beginning of a matter (especially the initial movement of business).
- **"Growing explosively"**: Rapid growth.
- **"Stagnating/Struggling to grow" (Nobinayamu)**: When growth slows down and becomes stagnant.
→ **The correct graph**: One that rises at a sharp angle at first and then becomes flat or shows a gentle decline.

## 🧠 5. EXAM LOGIC: Conquering the "Blind Spots" of Section 2

### ⚠️ Trap 1: The Unit Trap
The graph says **"Thousand Yen"** (Sen-en), but the audio talks in **"Ten Thousand Yen"** (Man-en). Or the graph is **"%"** (Percentage), but the audio describes **"Actual Numbers"** (Jissuu).
- **Strategy**: In the first 3 seconds of each question, always check the **Axis** and the **Unit**.

### ⚠️ Trap 2: Time Reversal
When the audio speaks of "this year's data" first and later compares it with "last year." Graphs usually flow from left to right (past to present).
- **Strategy**: Don't jump at the first number you hear; wait for contrast markers like **"Compared to this"** (Kore ni kurabete) or **"Last year"** (Sakunen wa) before making a judgment.

### ⚠️ Trap 3: Words Referring to Multiple Items
Expressions like **"Top 3 companies"** (Jou-i 3-sha) or **"Others"** (Sonota). It's important to note that the "Others" category can sometimes be larger than a specific individual item.
- **Strategy**: Concentrate on superlatives like **"Maximum" (Saidai)**, **"Minimum" (Saishou)**, or words expressing rank.

## 📝 6. QUICK QUIZ: Section 2 Breakthrough - Elite Challenge

**Q1:**
Which graph fits the explanation: "Initially, Product A was overwhelming (Attou) Product B, but entering the second half, the tide turned (**Keisei ga gyakuten**)?"
- A. A is higher than B throughout the year.
- B. B is higher than A throughout the year.
- C. A is higher in the first half, but B overtakes A in the second half.
- D. Sales of both products drop in the second half.

**Q2:**
What fact can be read from the statement: "Net sales decreased by 10% year-on-year, but the profit margin improved and has remained flat (**Yokobai**)?"
- A. Both sales and profit have dropped.
- B. Sales decreased, but the profit ratio has not changed (maintained the old level).
- C. Sales increased, and profit decreased.
- D. The company is in a state of serious deficit.

**Q3:**
In a pie chart of market share, if A (45%), B (30%), C (15%), and Others (10%), which is the correct J1+-level description?
- A. Company A alone has secured a majority.
- B. Even combining Company B and Company C, they do not reach Company A's share.
- C. Company A and Company B account for more than three-quarters of the market.
- D. "Others" has the largest share.

---

### 🔑 Answers & Explanations:

1. **C**: The keyword **"Gyakuten"** (Reversal) means a swap in ranking.
2. **B**: **"Yokobai"** means maintaining the status quo, showing that the profit margin (efficiency) is kept separate from the decrease in sales.
3. **C**: 45 + 30 = 75%, which is exactly **"three-quarters."** A has not exceeded 50%, so it is not a "majority."
